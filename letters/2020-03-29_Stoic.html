March 29, 2020 · Sunday

Stoic ?

10 photographs·412 words

If I get any thinking time during the week it is during my running
(shuffling) . Today I pondered why I am taking the current Flu season at
least semi-seriously.
That led to thinking about years past when I mostly ignored. I had the
Asian Flu when it went through in 1957. Remember it well. I think I am
pragmatic which might look fatalistic to " not from around here folks".

After I got home I spent some time reading and found the attached
interesting. I was surprised that the editor did not understand the
biblical foundation embedded in "good Lord willing and creeks don't rise."

https://blindpigandtheacorn.com/fatalism-in-appalachia/

We pretty much stayed in our undisclosed location all week but made one
outing to Erwin to aid and abet niece Lisa. On a previous trip she had
showed me some downed trees in the fence row and asked for help in removing
One was a rather large wild cherry I plan to take the truck back and
retrieve the logs.

We also consolidated the bamboo which had been scattered all about on the
last outing. If anyone needs bean poles I believe I know where they can be
obtained.

On the home front, the saga of the root ball is over.

The next chapter will be what, if anything, is to be done with the trunk.
Stay tuned.

The original objective for the week was start the '37. Close but no cigar.

Checked out the brakes which I believe are ok after replacing a bleed valve
missing from on rear brake drum.

Wayne came over to help check ignition system. After filing the points we
got a good spark at the plugs.

I rigged up my temporary fuel system ( but did not check, trying to start a
carburetor vehicle in the house is a fine way to burn the house down) so
all that is left is roll it out and fire it up. Stay tuned.

PBBD picked out some paint which she believes is close to original and
procured a spray can to do the part which will be under the to be
fabricated grill trim.

No work on the '63 but did move it off landing and put the on new fitted
cover.

At long last we are ready to find a new home for the Ford tractor.
