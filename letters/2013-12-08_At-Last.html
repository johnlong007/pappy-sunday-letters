December 8, 2013 · Sunday

At Last !

9 photographs·663 words

Early last Spring, I more or less finished work on the '37 and decided to
drag the MM in to work on until the weather got warm enough to apply
primer. Thursday it finally got warm enough so with the help of GSH primer
was applied.
I had about a pint of black primer which I used first ,covering the rear
left quarter. Then I had a gallon of gray which we used to cover the
balance and apply another coat to the black part.
Rain was projected for 3 PM. We started at 12 and did a good job for the
first coat, then it started sprinkling rain causing me to get in a hurry
making some runs in the second coat. But, overall quite pleased with the
results.
To top it off, D & D showed up right after we finished to do paint mixing
and matching for the MM.
Dwight cleaned the spray gun before they got on to mixing and matching.

.

Once more GSH got a call for what seems to be evolving as her culinary
specialty.

Meanwhile in case anyone is interested in what they old folks at home eat
when they do not go to BK

We did have an AT work day this week before it turned cold. Only a fey
workers turned out but we got quite a bit of maintenance and repair done.
We walked in on the blue trail to Laurel Falls then picked up the AT going
over Pond mountain. A tree had blown down causing hikers to have to cling
to a standing tree and edge around. We went in and dug behind the root ball
so hikers can pass behind. Tim Stewert is in the picture. He is our
youngest regular worker (Paul Benfield is oldest, almost a year older than
I but having serious health issues). A retired navy chief who was a mess
supply person.

In more catty news, GSH and the cat continue to be big buddies while I am
only making tentative progress. She can wag him around and I will not even
offer to pick him up.

I think my tombstone may read "Making do and Getting by." A replacement
lens for the missing headlight lens on the MM is on order. Turns out the
bulb is somewhat unique, at least in this day and age. Instead of a bayonet
type like a flashlight, the bulb base has a brass ring which is used to
lock the bulb in the socket. A replacement 6v build can be ordered for $30.
Saturday I sat in front of the fireplace. Took the remnants of the old bulb
apart, filled the tangs from an old 6V bulb I had thoughtfully stored in my
collection of "I may need this sometime" parts, slipped the retaining ring
on and tacked it with JB weld. I can scarce wait to try it. (It will have
to wait a few days as I took the only 6V battery i have out of the MM and
put it back in the '37)

We went to Northern Hydraulic where I procured a bed liner kit. I hope to
apply it either when weather warms up or GSH leaves long enough for me to
stink up the house and clear it before she returns. Then, I am going to
park the '37 until Summer to paint it and bring in the Winter project.

Not wanting to be caught with nothing to do, I have decided to make sure
the trailer is equipped to safely transport a five ton load. That means
brakes on all wheels, good tires and good wheel bearings. The trailer is
rated for six tons so the hitch, axles and frame should be adequate as is.
