January 29, 2012 · Sunday

Bi-Model

9 photographs·598 words

This week, I finished the course on Quantum Mechanics. Actually, what I did was finish viewing the 24 lectures. Now, I am going through the course outline trying to get the terms and definitions in mind. After that I plan (hope) to read the text then view the lecture series again. Notice that "hope" is a significant part of this plan.
On the other end of the spectrum, I worked on the brakes for the '37 pickup quite a bit.
If anyone would like to know what happens when a vehicle gets parked for years with brake fluid in the system, observe:

I did get all the wheel cylinders cleaned and honed. But, I do not have new cups to install. They use 1-1/4 inch cups in one end and 1-14 in the other.

GSH spent a lot of time and a lot of effort making squeaky toys for the dogs.

For diversion, I went with an old tractor expert to get Hal Ketron's tractor running for Sue. It must be running to sell and I took all the equipment needed to get it started. We hooked up the battery charger and got nothing. It was dead as a doornail and would not show any current flow. Finally gave up in despair. Sue came out and we told her she would need to purchase a new battery. She said her son in law had tried to charge it but it would not take a charge. She had tried to tell him there was something different about the way the battery is hooked up but she was not sure what. Then I understood why it was so dead and would not charge. The tractor is positive ground and he had hooked it backwards. I hope he did not cause voltage regulator and generator problems. May have to call in our electrical consultant.

THE CAR has remained right where I parked it last Sunday. My current plan is to put it in the shed and decide in the Spring time whether to get it out or leave it be. Open to requests and suggestion. Parental approval required for those not self supporting.

The weekly trip to Seymour was fun and rewarding. Pat has the chassis tack welded together. The primary objective was to weigh it and flip over so he can weld underneath.

Dwight came closest to guessing weight. I think he guessed 275 and it weighed in at 268.

But more important (if possible) we now have a drive sprocket for the M'cycle project and can get back to work . The old trail 90 rear sprocket and the new 200 cc engine sprocket do not take the same chain. That is a serious problem. We procured a correct chain sprocket then used the correct drive sprocket and persuaded the metal fabricators to take two and make one.

The weather has been lovely most of the week. AT work was at Hughes Gap again. It was cold and windy but just right for hard work. We had a big turnout of willing workers including two young ladies enticed in by Bob Peoples. Amy and Carol I believe. Amy is just back from six years in the South Pacific with the Peace Corp. Everyone who works a day gets a patch. Here Carl is awarding patches to the girls.

We built about 300 feet of new trail for the day.

So, while some are studying, some are defending our country and some are trying to make a living, some muddle (a technical term I learned while in the UK) through.
