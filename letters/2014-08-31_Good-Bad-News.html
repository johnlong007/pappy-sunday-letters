August 31, 2014 · Sunday

Good / Bad News

10 photographs·475 words

Bob ordered a new PC for me. Joe hooked it up and Alan got me started
moving things from the old to the new. The picture opening program is
different and I do not know how to use it. So, I think I will go with
attachments this week.

I went in for my 1,000 mile checkup on the hernia operation and got a full
release. Promised me that there would not be another on either side of
lower abdomen.

A number of exciting events this week:

Beth and Jake Jr. came to visit and eat B& G.

Alan and Catherine came in from the frozen North and brought Kenneth along
for the ride.

But most exciting of all ! The MM was started !!

I dragged in a diverse assortment of tractors about the time I was leaving
ECC. Wanted to make sure I would have fun things to occupy my time and
hands. I had never seen a MM in East Tn. but Dale knew of one which
belonged to one of his high school classmates having been purchased new by
the boys grandfather. It had quit running many years earlier and been
partial disassembled by the boy. I purchased it, brought it home and put in
the barn.

It was delight to finally get it running. However, it still has a fuel
problem and will only run a few seconds at a time. Much worse I expect is
that as I started pouring in expensive anti-freeze the radiator began to
leak profusely. Stay tuned.

Anita sponsored an event.

Beth came by with her budding tractor driver.

Meanwhile, back at the ranch, GSH continued working on her furniture.

And Anita to thrive on being a little ole grandmother.

AT work was fun. I have become the boulder and rock splitter for our crew.
None of them seen to be able to pick up the technique, All want to use
brute force and frail away with sledge hammers.
We are going to be putting in a new, multi lane bridge across the river
between Dennis Cove a Laurel Fork Gorge. To get the timbers to the bridge
site Carl wants a four foot wide road . Mostly pretty simple as the trail
follows an abandoned RR track out to the gorge. However, at one place, it
goes through a rock cut. Over time, a number of large rocks had fallen down
into the cut.

When I got there the guys were hammering away. The rocks were all
sedimentary with seams which one could discern with careful inspection.

One of the men I work with is building a house on a 5A tract he purchased
at Rocky Fork. Today we went to look it over. I really liked it about 20 by
20, one room with a 1/2 loft.

Running late so enough for now.
