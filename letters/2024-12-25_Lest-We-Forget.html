Lest We Forget

1 photographs·268 words

You should recognize my Uncle (by marriage) Ed Mashburn. My Aunt G was my
first and second grade teacher. 1941 and 1942. I remember being quite
confused when I had to quit calling her Miss Garland and switch to Miss
Mashburn' She went to Texas to see him after he finished basic training. I
clearly remember her bringing back a stalk of cotton for her class to see..

He waited until being drafted..After training, he and many other boys from
Tennessee were sent to Australia to rebuild the 32 INF division. A
Wisconsin National Guard unit. .. further information at this link:
https://www.nationalguard.mil/News/Article/2341531/the-red-arrow-fought-to-the-very-end-in-world-war-ii/

After he returned from his active duty, he took my brothers and I on
overnight fishing trips to the river 2 or 3 times each Summer..
Trotline fishing involves
a lot of sitting by a campfire talking and for me listening. As years went
by and I got older, I was able to ask more and learn more about his
experiences.

I learned about the first time he was shot at. The time a live grenade fell
in his foxhole but did not detonate.How to kill a person using a stick and
how to tell if a person is dead or faking.

Three stories I heard many times:.

The night of July 10,1944 when he faced the Japanese in defense of the
Drinimore. "Wake up Mashburn, they're coming.".

Christmas day (his birthday) 1944 when his unit crossed the Limon river

The fighting he was involved in on the Villa Verde trail.

Further information in two attached files, excerpts from book "General
Arthur's Report)
