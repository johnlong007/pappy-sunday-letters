October 18, 2009 · Sunday

Fluff

7 photographs·424 words

Every since the Dr. sliced and diced on me (four months back) I have not
been able to do my running around at the same level I could before this all
started.This week, I experience a marked change. No attempt at timing but I
believe I am almost back to "normal". Outside of running everything has
been back to normal for a while. So far, so good.

My work buddy Dave had heart speed control problems and was in VA hospital
for a couple of days. We should get back to do-gooder before long.

Hal is getting slower and more worn down. But, still Hal.

The AT crew is planning to replace the curly maple shelter (in Unicoi
county between the river and Beauty Spot). This week, we cut all the pieces of
wood so that only assembly will be required on site. Now looking at early
November for actual construction.

It appears we have a group of four male turkeys (Aaron, it is bow season)
and a group of five female turkeys hanging around. It appears the turkeys
like the young Rye sprouts almost as much as the seeds. They also like the
persimmons very well. Not sure I need to include a picture as they look
about the same as before. But, I will include a critter picture. I sure would
like a big pot of squirrel gravy. GSH has offered to make the gravy if she is
supplied with cleaned ingrediants.
We had five under the feeder the other day so if anyone wants to harvest it
would be welcomed.

Good progress on the car project.

I should make a list of the component sources.
Studebaker body shell, Chrysler engine, transmission and front half of the
driveshaft, Fiat steering column, Alfa pedals, Honda clutch MC and Dodge
brake MC, Ford running gear.
I sorted through all the parts and find I am still missing at least four
needed pieces. So, I suppose I will go harass JW once more before I give up
and try to purchase. Stay tuned.

Some sort of invasive plant it taking the place. It has about crowded out
the grass under the walnut trees. GSH picked up sticks and limbs so we could
mow it in hopes of slowing the invasion. The turkeys watched with interest.

In spite of the critters, we have a good stand of Rye which is now a couple
of inches high. Just right for plucking. I suppose when it gets a bit
higher the deer will decide to help control growth.
