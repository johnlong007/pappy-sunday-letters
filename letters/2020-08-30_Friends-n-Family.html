August 30, 2020 · Sunday

Friends 'n Family

12 photographs·471 words

Today we went to Perkins for breakfast (GSH always has a $4 muffin) then to
church, then to SS !. About 1/4 of the regulars were there. And, somewhat
to my surprise, the ones I know to be high risk were the ones in
attendance. Maybe the Sturgis effect.

As people often do, PBBD had a birthday this week.She did not even ask me
to retell the story of our last week in Knoxville. Available upon request.

Wayne cut down the dead pine which was bugging neighbor Larry (who in turn
was bugging me). He notched properly, hinge worked perfect and tree fell
just where it was aimed.

Ted Mowery is one of the folks with whom I worked on the AT, Christopher,
Benj and Andrew have met him, maybe some others. Ted is perhaps the highest
at risk person I know.
His wife brought him and he delivered peppers and tomatoes to us. I was
humbled.

Today, Meredith showed up. Fresh, hot, homemade muffins. Better than
Perkins !. I had moved the '63 out for Diane to inspect. Tried to persuade
Meredith to take it for a spin but she declined.

Back to the friends, Don and Nancy came by with a big box of tomatoes and
cucumbers. In addition, Nancy had made a baked spaghetti dish for us. For
about one week/year I usually have more tomatoes than I can eat. I believe
this is the week.

Aaron and Chelsea were in town for a brief stop. My mother would pinch
child's cheek and say "Not missed many meals".

Thomas more or less offered to buff the '63. I suspect it is quite a bit
more than he recognized. Anyway, PBBD is my designated QA inspector. My
interpretation of her findings is: miles to go before he rests.

Current status of the famous dead pine tree. Once more, I will need help as
neither the tractor nor Bobcat can handle without tearing up the lawn.

This picture is an "after" shot of the truck anti-roll (sway) bar. It had
pulled a large rusted away section from the frame. Anyone looking for a
good paying meaning full career should look at welding. $600.

GSH and I dug the rotten wood out of the hollow logs which have been
delivered to Anita with the expectation she can do something creative with
them.

Laura asked about hickory wood. I told her I believed we had a dead hickory
out near the ramp. I cut it, sawed it up after which she determined it to
be Ash. Open further looking, I believe the source of the hickory nuts I
had been finding is this tree located across the drive.
