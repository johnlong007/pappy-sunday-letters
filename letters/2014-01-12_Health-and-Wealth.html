January 12, 2014 · Sunday

Health and Wealth

6 photographs·584 words

I suppose everyone is waiting with baited breath for a report from the cat
house? So, we will get that over with first thing.

Moving on to the next dangling participle, when the Dr. worked on my head
he cut a large, round plug out. He then when in and cut out a pie shaped
piece on edge side (picture upon request) so he could pull the scalp fore
and aft to cover over the gap. Because of all the pulling he had to sew the
sides together with baler twine and will not remove it for another two
weeks. Actually Jan. 24th. I am not looking forward to that as expect the
stitches will have to be grubbed by then. In the meantime, back at the
ranch< GSH is keeping the wound cleaned and covered with Vaseline.

It does not hurt much except when I bump my head which brings up the next
topic.

It was cold early this week so I stayed indoors and worked on painting the
MM , sanding on the LBT and then started on the brakes on the LBT.
Painting on the MM went along very well, It took me almost two days.I also
used up all but 1/2 pint of the custom mixed paint provided by D & D.
Fortunately, I did get some help on some detail work which I greatly
appreciated.

Overall, I would rate it as about a 25 footer, pretty good for painting
with a brush.

I mentioned brakes on the LBT, of course bleeding the wheel cylinders
requires doubling over and sticking ones head inside the wheel well. In
which case, it must eventually be extracted normally accompanied by bumping
back of head into inside of whell cut out.
Even worse is the master cylinder.

Brake fluid is hygroscopic, it absorbs moisture out of the air up to a
level of about 3% by volume as I remember. During normal operation braking
generates enough heat to keep the moisture evaporated. However, a vehicle
parked for an extended period with fluid in the system will get the fluid
saturated with water. Master cylinders and wheel cylinders being made of
cast iron rust, corrode and pit. Never in history has it been possible to
pour fluid into a system allowed to sit for years and have the brakes work.
But, I though it might this time so I poured fluid into the master
cylinder. The result being that while I was flat on my back knocking dust
and rust into my face a eyes I was also getting brake fluid dripped into my
face and eyes. All the while bumping my head on a concrete floor every time
I moved.

But, After about four hours effort, the MC is out!

Can you imagine how much sympathy I got from GSH?

On the way home from breakfast with the GOBs on Saturday morning, I stop at
McD's and get a country ham biscuit and a parfait for GSH.

Too cold for AT work this week, We are scheduled to go out again this
Tuesday if anyone wants to tag along.

We are cutting into our winter wood supply.

I would guess 4-5 weeks left with no reserve for next year. May have to
call in some summer campers this summer.

We may get cold but should be able to obtain food.
