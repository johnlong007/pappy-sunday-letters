June 2, 2014 · Monday

Victory

13 photographs·486 words

Linda took Adam and Sarah on a victory lap Last week. They stopped by here
coming and going.

While we working on the AT Thursday at Ash Gap, I harvested a sack full of
mature ramps. So, we fed Linda and kids fried potatoes and ramps.

​

​

​

​
>From the top of Roan Mountain the AT drops down (or up going North) four
miles to Hughes Gap.. Planning a relocation started seven years ago with
actual work starting five years ago (Takes a while to get shovel ready).
The AT is maintained by free labor but falls under supervision of the US
Forest Service.

Thursday we officially finished the project and had a grand opening
ceremony!

​
We do not use power equipment (chain saws) when we have a large group of
inexperienced people. So, for the last big outing a couple of weeks back a
number of trees were left to be removed Thursday. One of our newer workers
(Jim Chambers) has gone through certification training but has no practice
so Carl sent me to go with him to remove the designated trees.

​
We did just that. Jim is a big, former marine. He also won the best beard
contest at recent trail days event. He can handle the saw as if it is a
toy.

The relocation involved a total of about 30 switchbacks (zig zags) .It will
only be appreciated by those who have climbed the old trail.

Practice continued with most recent student driver. In making sharp turns
in the truck moaning and groaning sounds were heard. Turns out it was from
the vehicle. It has a horizontal shock absorber on the steering tie rod to
prevent steering shimmy. Some how, the rod had become bent.

​
After considerably work it came out but the pressed in retaining stud
remains in place. To be continued. Which reminds me, I keep getting "buy
local;" admonitions. Buying the oven heating element local rather than
on-line came at twice the price $30 vs. $15.

The old folks at home are muddling along. It appears the cat is assuming a
dominate role.

​

​

​Some went for a moonlight cruise on the HMS D & D. Duck spotting was a a
big attraction.

​
Speaking for duck spotting. I spotted a momma turkey with a big group of
turklets wandering across the field. By the time I got my camera they were
about out of view.

​
Wayne decided a couple of trees in his front yard should be downed and I
got called in as an interested observer. Knowing George had procured an
expensive pole saw, I thought it might fit into the job and asked George to
bring it by for a test run.

​
Other exciting events transpared I am sure but those will be left for
members of the extended family to reveal (or not) as they see fit.
