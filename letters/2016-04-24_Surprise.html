April 24, 2016 · Sunday

Surprise !

10 photographs·488 words

Monday GSH and I walked our section of the AT once more. Going North as
usual to remove a reported blowdown. Probably a dozen hikers passed us. One
was actually going South, a flip flopper.

In due time, we found a smart phone in the trail I picked it up and
mustered full speed to try to catch the last hiker. Fortunately they had
stopped for lunch. It belonged to a young lady who was very appreciative.
We removed the obstruction and passed them once more as we headed back.
As all know, I have had the '63 in the shop working mostly under it for the
last few months. Flat on my back getting covered with dirt, oil, dust, rust
and undercoating. Recently GSH showed me a pillow case and tactfully
suggested I should wash my head better and more often. In the spirit of
locking the barn door, I moved the car out and gave it a good pressure
washing. Also plan to finish undercoating while it is outside. It has been
(also tactfully) suggested that it would be better to do the undercoating
outside as not everyone enjoys the odor.

For some time I have been fretting about the remaining portion of the Elm
tree resting on the Ag building roof. One day the planets lined up and a
helper showed up . With the aid on the helper and the Bobcat the tree trunk
was removed without accident or injury. Note the restraining harness and
the left handed sawyer.

The Gray station old tractor (owners) show was this week. I worked the
registration 1/2 day Wed. and all day Th-Sat. It is a lot of fun talking
with tractor people from all over this side of the Mississippi with quite a
spread in age of owners and tractors. Also a lot of vendors selling a lot
of "stuff". I offered $40 for a kettle . He wanted $85. Not quite sure what
it was made for but believe it was a jam kettle.

One of the more interesting demonstrations was a mule power corn sheller.

Another item I had not seen before was a split exhaust manifold 8N. Jake
might be interested. He said he paid $400.

Today, we went to Erwin. Lisa is adapting to the life of the landed gentry
including the owes thereof. Not sure whether I did her any favors or not.
She had been told the tractor and bush hog had various ailments. I checked
it over, made a couple of passes and told her all the ailments I could find
were about normal for the age and mileage to go ahead and mow.

Then we dragged out a push mower but could not get it running. I believe it
needs a carburetor diaphragm . Then dragged out the powered drive mower
and got it fired right up. So, she will be able to mow to her hearts
content.
