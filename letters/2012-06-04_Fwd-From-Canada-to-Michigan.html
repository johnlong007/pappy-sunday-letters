I am due an update, and as always, I figure I will address this to the two
people who I know want it and let them forward it to anyone else who might.
I'll try to attach enough pictures to make up for the length.

As some of you know, I'm working at Michigan State University for the
summer. I went to MSU for a conference earlier in the year, which helped
line things up for me to come back this summer. I start tomorrow, working
at the National Superconducting Cyclotron Laboratory on campus. It's a very
nice and very big campus-- which you might have guessed by the fact that it
has it's own cyclotron, and according to US News and World Report, it's the
top ranked nuclear physics school in the country. I'm paired up with an
advisor who is a professor here and have been assigned a specific project
assembling and testing a gas detector for nuclear physics reactions. There
are 11 other students working on other projects.

On to more interesting things. One of my friends from school has been
desperately trying to get a road trip together, so I told him anything that
landed me in Michigan today would work very well for me, and I would
contribute the money I get from the program for travel towards gas. So we
took a five day journey through Canada, and he dropped me of in East
Lansing yesterday. We didn't do a terribly large amount of planning, but
were consistently able to overcome that through good fortune and nice
people.

We left Sunday afternoon and drove all the way to Erie, Pennsylvania, about
11 hours or so. We got in after 3 am and set up our little tent in a likely
full RV campground. We checked in the next morning. The attendent laughed
and told us he'd take $10. We walked around the state park on the cost of
lake Erie for awhile and then went on to Niagara. Niagara Falls is a sight
to behold. Niagara, NY and Niagara, ON are not. It's basically upscale
Gatlinburg. We spent most of our time on the New York side, which has
established a state park that essential keeps the cost from the clutter the
Canadian side has. Its a shame to see such commercialism built up around a
marvel of nature. Signs everywhere tell you you'll be prosecuted for
getting in the water. We got screamed at a few times for dipping our toes
in. I can't imagine how anyone can think nature is experienced by the
standing at a reasonable distance and taking a picture. We did take the
boat ride down up to the falls, which was quite worth it. That evening we
backtracked 30 minutes and had buffalo wings in Buffalo, NY at the bar that
invented them. Quite tasty. We crossed the border and had enough light to
see the falls from the Canadian side, which has the better view. We did get
the entirety of our stuff searched inside and out when we crossed the
border. I don't think they were totally sold on two people driving into
their country without specific plans, and were visibly confused as to how
anyone could be carrying that many clothes, physics textbooks, and opera
DVDs on a 5 day camping trip.

We spent the night at another camping sight, and were awoken by a
construction worker about to pave the area we slept in. When we talked to
the people at check in the lady asked if we were the people who almost got
run over by a bulldozer. I said I wasn't sure, did we? We were asleep. She
laughed and said we didn't need to pay anything. We spent the next day in
Toronto. It's a fine city. The CN tower was predictable an expensive
tourist trap, and I probably would recommend others to skip it. A Blue Jays
game in the evening was a relaxing end to a long day. We planned to find a
place to stay halfway between Toronto and our next stop that night, but had
a terrible time finding anything. Canada is expensive, and even the
cheapest, sketchiest hotels were $70. We kept driving and finding nothing
until we ended up at our final destination at 4 in the morning. We found a
place that looked like it was for only RV's, but wasn't blocked off, and
slept there just when we were convinced we'd be sleeping in the car in
walmart's parking lot.

The man that owned the place was very nice. Georgian Bay Islands National
Park is a collection of some of the main islands in the over 30,000 islands
in the georgian bay. The park is only accessible by water. The ferry for
the park was closed but the man we stayed with gave us a fine deal to take
us in his boat that day and pick us up in the morning. We were a bit scared
when two people warned us their stuff had been ripped apart by bears the
last time they camped there, but they offered to let us keep our cooler on
their boat for the night. I also forgot my sleeping bag that night, and was
very, very cold. No bears though.

The next day we went to Bruce Peninsula National Park, which is comprised
of a large portion of the Bruce Peninsula that sticks out into the Georgian
bay. Look at a map of Ontario if you're interested. It was beautiful. Lots
of rocky costs and big cliffs, which we had a great time climbing around
on. We did get in the water, which was so cold it made your body numb after
about 10 seconds. I insisted that we get in though, and I think we would
have regretted it had we not. We didn't stay in a lot longer than 10
seconds though. It was much deeper than it appeared, as it's very very
clear. The sunset was also gorgeous, see pictures. We'd hoped to go to the
Marine park right next door the next day, but it rained and all boat rides
were closed. It's a shame, as the area is famous for shipwrecks. The very
cold water has preserved the ruins for hundreds of years, making the area
very popular with scuba divers. There are glass bottom boats which give you
a chance to see the shipwrecks, but unfortunately these weren't running.

The last day we went to Point Pelee National Park, which is a small
peninsula that comprises the southernmost tip of Canada. We then went into
Detroit and watched a soccer game. We tried to get tickets to the Detroit
Tigers game, but the Yankees were in town and all but two tickets that were
expensive and not together were sold out. The let me check in a day early
at MSU yesterday, so we relaxed in my dorm room here. Nash left this
morning and drove back to Nashville, I'm here until August 10.

I'm having a terrible time compressing pictures, but I will send a few in a
bit in some format when I get it settled.
