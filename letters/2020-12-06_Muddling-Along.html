December 6, 2020 · Sunday

Muddling Along

10 photographs·412 words

We came up the drive one evening and found a vehicle here. A well masked
man holding a nice plastic bag which he announced was a Christmas gift from
our church to the church shut ins. That was a shock to my system. Still is.
However, in line with my policy, I was able to take the bag and say thank
you.

Christopher and Mary Allison came by. I do believe the '37 is a real "chick
magnet". It was a delightful visit. At least for me.

Muddling through is a British expression. It means carry through to a
successful conclusion in spite of any apparent plan or direction.

The big project for the week was getting operational brakes on the '37 PU.
I think we muddled through.
As you may recall I was whimpering because wheel cylinders are $70. In
essence I worked a week for $280. However, that is after tax.
Below is a picture of my basket of brake parts from years and vehicles
past. MDGB. (Make Do, Get By, the story of my life.)

Honed and polished all the old wheel cylinders. Installed the best of the
used innards. Conscripted Wayne to help with one wheel which would not
bleed.

Bleeding brakes is a two person task which requires being synchronized.
Mash, hold, release. On command !

First snow of the season, almost enough to bring the canopy down. So,
yesterday we took it down.

Some time back I had taken the big trailer to Erwin for a project. Thinking
I would need the trailer again before long, I left it parked out front.
Now, with weather and other issues to cope with I decided to put it back in
storage. But first GSH more or less agreed to sweep off the leaves.

Sunday, a no work day, we went for a walk . A nice scenic walk.

This is to show I am not always leaning on GSH. She is enjoying a cup of
Christmas present hot chocolate from a grandson present cup.

I suppose everyone has some childhood memories which stick out. This
cartoon brought one back to me. My father was working on the distributor in
the 49 Dodge. I was holding light. He stopped handed me the wrench and said
here, you do the job and I will shine the light in your eyes.
