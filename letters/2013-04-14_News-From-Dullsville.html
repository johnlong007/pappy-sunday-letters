April 14, 2013 · Sunday

News From Dullsville

10 photographs·456 words

The big news, actually the only news, is that the '37 lives again. My
father purchased a 1937 pickup truck new in 1937 and kept it until 1949.
Of course, Herb and Hazel remember it better than I do. I never drove it on
the road, only in the fields.
Somewhere along the way I decided I really wanted/needed a '37 pickup and
in time found one listed in Hemmings. We (all eight of us) loaded up and
went to Fairbault, MN. to claim our treasure. We towed it home with the
little blue truck.That would have been in the early 70's. It was running
when we got it but I never drove it at all.
I worked on it some but lost heart for it when Daddy died and parked it
until this Winter. So, it had been about 40 years since it had been run.
I did learn one thing yesterday, spray adhesive does not work well as
starting fluid.

The signs were right for root crop planting this week. Potatoes (Red,
yellow and white, should be ready about July 4th), carrots, beets and
radishes.

I worked on the AT both Monday and Tuesday. Monday was helping cut
blowdownson brother Dave's section at Wilbur dam and Tuesday was
working at White
Rock roughing out zig zags up the backbone of a ridge getting work lined
out for the "Hardcore" crew.
Each year we recruit about 100 willing workers during the Damascus Trail
Days .Then. we have work for them on Sunday, Monday and Tuesday after trail
days. It takes a lot of planning to have work ready to keep 100 people busy
for three days.
Of course, while I was out, I checked on the ramp status. Looks like a good
year but another 2-3 weeks before serious harvesting should start.
For the last 2-3 years I have been nipping the ends from ramps which I
clean here. Then, I plant the nipped root ends. This year, I see signs of
actual spreading of the patches (I have four areas planted). Meredith may
be able to harvest some here next year.
The lawn birds are out in force. I took a lot of pictures of birds at the
feeder. Most of the birds I can identify. One I could not.
Most mundane of all, the mowing season has arrived. The string trimmer,
push mower and rider all started with little difficulty. A good thing I
enjoy being outside.
We went out to the hillside today to deliver some dead car parts to Dwight.
While there, I took a picture of the hill where we planted pines and where
we planted poplars long ago. Do the involved parties remember? That was in
1967 and 1968.
