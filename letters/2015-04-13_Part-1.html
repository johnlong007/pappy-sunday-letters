April 13, 2015 · Monday

Part 1

6 photographs·404 words

Went with Herb back to Vanderbilt on Monday. This time to talk with an
oncologist. I found her to be quite impressive.

1. His tumor was large and embedded in bone and tissue. Very unlikely it
was all excised.
2. He can wait and see if it recurs. She talked about signs and portends.
3. Best option is a combination of focused radiation and chemotherapy.

She will develop a plan go over with his Dr. in Johnson City. If local Dr.
agrees treatment can be here. Otherwise, back to Vanderbilt for seven
weeks/five days/week. He should hear this week and will then have to decide.

We stopped to visit Herb's son James, wife Lisa and all the little noodniks
on the way out of Nashville. A delightful family . I thought for a while I
was going to be able to borrow "a sweet little girl" like I used to have.

Back in River City, I pulled the oil pump from the LBT. Sure enough, the
pressure control valve was (and is) stuck tighter than Dick's hatband. I am
told Wayne has procured a replacement oil pump and pan gasket. Stay tuned.

We had company coming on Friday so that presented a good reason for a big
Spring cleanup. Inside and outside. Biggest improvement inside was/is in
the utility room. Hauled a bunch of surplus artifacts away.

On the first bike trip GSH took to Europe, she borrowed a bike from Herb.
When I was over to visit, he offered it to me for GSH to bring back to
life. She took it for a test run. She has always been a very cautious
person. My theorem about getting old is one does not change , one just gets
more the way they are. She proved that on her test ride, there is a limit
as to how slow one can ride a bike on a gravel road.

One day the rains came down and the floods came up. When things dry out
(and the signs are right) I am going to brings the crawler over from Herbs
and use it and the Bobcat to build a small pond at upper end of the soccer
field. This time, I ma going to install a liner.

Some. at least one, may be interested in knowing that ramps are "in". I
have planted several patches here and signs are they are growing and
spreading although slowly.
