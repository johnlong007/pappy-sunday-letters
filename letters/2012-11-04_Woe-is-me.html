November 4, 2012 · Sunday

Woe is me

7 photographs·512 words

Not a bit sure I made the world a better place this week.

I did a fair job visiting the halt, lame and afflicted and made a run to
Seymour to check progress on the race car.No work on the AT due to the
weather and no Friday hike due to inertia.

My friends (not to mention family) are getting broke down and stove up. One
of the AT workers ( Paul Benfield, one year older than I am) had a blood
loss problem for some time. A couple of weeks back he had his colon
removed. I have been to see him a couple of times out near Lamar community
in JC. On the way over Monday the traffic was backed up just before exit
ramp. I took a picture as going by , very slowly going by.
We brought some liquor filled chocolate cherries back from Germany. I
thought I would do Paul a real favor and took him a couple. He had told me
he could eat anything he wanted except popcorn or nuts. I gave him the
cherries, he unwrapped one, bit into it, said he recognize the taste, went
to the door and spit it out. I guess he has reformed from all the stories
he told me about being stationed in Germany while in the service. I was
crushed.

Another friend, Larry Kidd has been in the hospital for over two weeks in a
drug induced comma suffering from Wegeners disease. A big old burly 65 year
old I thought was indestructible. We have been to see Debby every couple
of days. She stays there all day, every day.

One evening when I came in there was a big flock of turkeys by the drive,
probably 30 or more. By the time I got the camera ready they had headed for
the pine woods.

Kim talked with the car dealer and had her car hauled in and checked. Sure
enough, a new transmission for $3,300. Keep that in mind when you are
buying a car.

I spent all my discretionary time working on the '37. Got a few things
done, the drive shaft and the belly pans installed. I did NOT get the
voltage regulator wired. The voltage regulator has 5 terminals but wiring
diagram only show four. So, I am stymied. I suspect it is not the original
voltage regulator.

The trip to Seymour was fun. I had not seen the car for a few weeks. The
chrome-moly frame is finished. Pat had finished engine assembly so the task
was fit the engine, set the body on and install the dry sump oil system.
Fitting the engine and body took about 10 minutes, the dry sump oil
reservoir took several hours of mostly pondering by Dwight, Pat and Jack.
Sanford and I watched and nodded wisely.

My giant screen TV/monitor ceased and desisted. After a great deal of
stomping and fussing hurting feelings right and left, I contacted Samsung
help. Very nice but did not resolve the problem. Not sure what I will do
next.
