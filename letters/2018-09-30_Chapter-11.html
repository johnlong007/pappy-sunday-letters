September 30, 2018 · Sunday

Chapter 11

15 photographs·639 words

Debra snow came up to GSH and me after early church service this morning. I
knew she had an important announcement, much more excited than the last
announcement , something about Ross and Katy.

This time she wanted to tell us Ross had killed a 4 point buck! He had to
drag it out then his Dad and two others carried it out.

I hope Katy is able to fill in more details and perhaps with a picture or
two.

In less exciting news, a couple of weeks back I noticed the auto storage
building showing signs of moisture damage along the front edge. David
Quillan cave me the name of a roofer he believed to be both honest and
competent. Wayne called and had him come out to inspect the roof.

While he was here, I asked him to inspect the barn and lean to roof. The
barn roof has been leaking for years but he said not mush damage to the
internal structure. The shed roof is not leaking but years of being wet
under leaves and pine needles have caused the tin to rust severely.

Several years back ,time flies, the lean to had filled up and I decided to
extend it I had timber sawed and graded out the site.

GSH said "Why are you doing that? It will just fill up also". So I dropped
that project and forgot all about it.

Time marched on and the site grew up with Jack pines.

Most of the week was spent clearing the site and cleaning the roof. I could
not keep this place beat into submission without my all purpose mower.

One tree I decided to cut was leaning over the shed and too big for the
mower. So, I hooked the tractor to it with a quite long chain.

When the tree fell, It brought another much smaller on with it. The bowed
tree is a number one danger for a sawyer, a springpole . If not done
properly, one can lose their head.
Anyone know how to cut safely? If you don't, then don't try it at home.

The finished product. Ready for the project cost estimate.

In other exciting news, Qwendolyn flew in for a visit. A high school friend
of hers, and daughter of parents with whom I worked at ECC, asked her to a
bridesmaid. I was very pleased that she used part of her very brief visit
time to come visit the ole folks at home. Equally pleasing she made B&G,
making biscuits from scratch just as her Grandmother had taught her. The
biscuits were just as good. Delightful and delicious.

Anyone care for a Ground Hog stuffed with turnips? Free to good home. All
you have to do is capture it.

Alan and Catherine came by, they were here from the frozen North for
another family event. They also decided to visit the OFAH. But, did no
cooking.
We did tour the place and Catherine tried a Paw paw. Chestnut trees are
loaded. Lisa reported Ella saw a bear heading for her chestnut trees on
day. Better the chestnuts than the goat.
The turkeys and deer work on the fruit and nuts here, The deer stomp and
chomp the chestnuts then the turkeys clean up the scraps. Turkeys also like
persimmons and we have a bumper crop.

I almost forgot . We had an event.I will leave it to others to post
pictures. All our children, many of the grandchildren ,I did not count, ALL
the GGC plus two pending GGC. several spouses and lots of others.

Where do cats sleep. In drawers (literally).
