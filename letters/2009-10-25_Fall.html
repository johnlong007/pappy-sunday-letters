October 25, 2009 · Sunday

Fall !

5 photographs·477 words

Let's see, I have told you about the turkeys, the deer, the possums and the
coons. No point in sending pictures as they look just as before.

I fiddled with The Studebaker quite a bit. Dwight worked on it also. The
clutch is rigged. Gas tank is in but no lines or pump. Brakes are going to
require some work. Turns out, JW had purchased used running gear. The brakes
had been left with fluid in the wheel cylinders . So, the wheel cylinders
must be overhauled. Front springs are also installed. We decided the old
ones we had installed were not heavy duty enough so Dwight ordered new ones.
Don't think there is any chance the front end will sag.

Health and wealth, I have learned by experience that sugar and exercise do
not mix. We eat breakfast at Perkins' on Sunday mornings before church.
Last Sunday, I ate three pan cakes with syrup. We went up to the track as soon
as we got home from church. I made it three laps and had to quit and go
home.As if that was not enough, GSH made an all edge pineapple upside down
cake. Of course, I ate too much every day. Net result was I did terrible
running until Thursday. Pretty good since then.

We went by to see Hal but they were not home. I expect that is a good sign.

AT work Tuesday was at the Curly Maple shelter. It is about 1/2 way between
Chestoa (where we use to go fishing in the gorge) and Indian Grave Gap
(where the road past Rock Creek Park crossed into NC). They put me and my
swamper out at Chestoa then drove around to Martin's creek and walked in. I
carried the big saw and cut blowdowns on the way to the shelter. It is about 3
1/2 miles about 1/2 of that fairly steep uphill. I made it very well, not
fast but ok. Had to cut 15 downed trees. Most of them large 12-18 inches.

We are going to replace the shelter. It is the one we were cutting parts
for last week. This week part of the crew was clearing the logging road
access route to bring in the materials while Ed and Bruce were deciding what
could be salvaged from the existing shelter.
We ran into a snag. There is a big oak tree just behind the shelter. Of
course. it will fall sometime in the future. When it does, it will smash the
shelter (and any occupants).We debated about cutting it, asking the forest
service to cut it, or leave it for future maintainers to deal with. Stay
tuned.

On the home front, the leaves are full pretty, I finished mowing and
cleaning under the walnuts so that looks good and we replaced the mail box with
a larger one.
