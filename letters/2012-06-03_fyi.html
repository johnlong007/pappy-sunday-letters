Refitting and retraining

After the 32nd Division wrapped up operations at Buna, the men were slowly
transported to Australia for rest, rehabilitation and training. The first
units arrived in Brisbane, Australia on 1 March 1943. The complete move
took several weeks because of the limited number of planes available to
transport the troops over the Owen Stanley Mountains and the few ships
available to get the men from Port Moresby to Australia. The last units
arrived in April.[47]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-training-46>
Upon
arrival, some men spent a few days camped near Brisbane and went into town
to get drunk. The division then returned to Camp Cable where it had been
stationed before it left for New
Guinea.[47]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-training-46>

On 1 March 1943, Major General William H.
Gill<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=William_H._Gill&action=edit&redlink=1>
at
Camp Carson, Colorado was ordered to Australia to assume command of the
division. After a period of rest, the division began training to
incorporate the many replacements into its ranks and help them gain the
lessons of jungle warfare they had gained in battle. During March, the
107th Quartermaster Battalion was reorganized as the 32nd Quartermaster
Company, and the ordnance detachment was redesignated the 732nd Ordnance
Company. The 121st Field Artillery Battalion's 155 mm howitzers were
replaced with 75 mm howitzers. By October 1943 the Division was ready for
combat and moved back to New
Guinea.[47]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-training-46>
From
16 to 30 October 1943 the 32nd Division was moved from Australia back to
New Guinea. At Milne Bay and Goodenough Island they continued their
training and prepared for future combat
operations.[47]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-training-46>

During December 1943 the U.S. Sixth Army was given the mission to capture
Saidor with the goal to cut off the Japanese retreat from Finschhafen. The
32nd division formed the majority of the Michaelmas Task Force given the
assignment to help establish a blocking position at Saidor with the intent
to trap an entire Japanese division at Sio. They were charged with seizing
the airfield at Saidor and securing the surrounding area. The main combat
power for the task force was the 126th
Infantry.[47]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-training-46>
[edit
]Operation Cartwheel
Main article: Operation
Cartwheel<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cartwheel>

<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Troops_of_the_32d_Division_near_Saidor.jpg>
32nd Division troops cross a stream near Saidor.

On 2 January 1944, the 32nd Division took part in Operation
Cartwheel<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cartwheel>,
part of MacArthur's "leap-frog" operational plan to take strategic points
to use as forward bases. MacArthur sent Allied forces westward along the
1,500 miles (2,400 km) northern coast of New Guinea in a sequence of
operations against selected locations that would provide landing fields for
aircraft. This was designed to provide his ground forces with continued close
air support <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_air_support>and deny the
enemy sea and airborne resupply, effectively cutting the Japanese forces
off as they were under
attack.[48]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-47>
[49]

Elements of the 32nd's 126th Infantry Regimental Combat
Team<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/126th_Cavalry_Regiment_%28United_States%29>
 landed at Saidor <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_at_Saidor> on the
north coast of New Guinea and helped to end enemy resistance there on 14
April 1944.[50]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-Miller_p296-49>
On
23 April, elements took part in the landing at
Aitape<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_at_Aitape>,
the division arriving on 3 May. After meeting slight initial resistance,
the 32nd had to withstand savage counterattacks in the Driniumor
River<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Driniumor_River>
 area.[51]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-50>

By 31 August, Aitape was secured and the division rested. Elements landed
on Morotai <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Morotai> on 15
September. The 32nd's command post opened at
Hollandia<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayapura>
,Dutch New Guinea <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_New_Guinea> on 1
October, setting the stage for the advance into the Philippines.
[edit<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29&action=edit§ion=25>
]Philippines battles[edit<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29&action=edit§ion=26>
]Battle of Leyte
Main article: Battle of Leyte <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte>

The Battle of Leyte <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte> was the invasion
and conquest of Leyte in the Philippines by American and Filipino guerrilla
forces under the command of MacArthur. They fought against Imperial
Japanese Army <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army> forces
in the Philippines led by General Tomoyuki
Yamashita<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoyuki_Yamashita>.
The battle took place from 17 October to 31 December 1944 and launched the
Philippines campaign of 1944–45, the goal of which was to recapture and
liberate the entire Philippine
Archipelago<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Archipelago> and
to end almost three years of Japanese occupation. The 32nd Division landed
on Leyte <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte> on 14 November, and went into
action along thePinamopoan<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pinamopoan&action=edit&redlink=1>
-Ormoc <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormoc> highway, taking
Limon<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Limon%2c_Leyte&action=edit&redlink=1>
and
smashing the Yamashita
line<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Yamashita_line&action=edit&redlink=1>
in
bitter hand-to-hand combat. The division linked up with elements of the U.S.
1st Cavalry Division<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Cavalry_Division_%28United_States%29>
in
the vicinity ofLonoy<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lonoy&action=edit&redlink=1>,
on 22 December, marking the collapse of Japanese resistance in the upper Oromoc
Valley <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormoc>.
[edit
]Battle of Luzon
Main article: Battle of Luzon <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Luzon>

>From Leyte the Division moved to Lingayen
Gulf<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingayen_Gulf>,
Luzon, on 27 January 1945. It pushed up the Villa
Verde<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Verde> Trail,
on 30 January, and after 119 days of fighting, it took
Imugan<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Imugan&action=edit&redlink=1>
.[52]
Four
men were awarded the Medal of Honor during its six-week fight up the
20 miles of the Villa Verde Trail: William R.
Shockley<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_R._Shockley>
, David M. Gonzales <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_M._Gonzales>, Thomas
E. Atkins <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_E._Atkins>, and Ysmael R.
Villegas <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ysmael_R._Villegas>. It took
American troops until the end of June to seize the Cagayan
Valley<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley> and
its food supplies.

On 28 May it met the 25th Infantry
Division<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29>
 near Santa Fe<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Santa_Fe%2c_Philippines&action=edit&redlink=1>,
securing Balete, Aklan <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balete%2c_Aklan> Pass,
the gateway to the Cagayan <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan> Valley.
While elements of the division continued mopping-up activities near Imugan,
other units moved to rest and rehabilitation centers. Active elements
secured the Baguio <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio> area, wiped out
Japanese forces in the Agno River
<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River> Valley
area, and opened Highway
11<http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Highway_11_%28Philippines%29&action=edit&redlink=1>
as
a supply route. They captured General Tomoyuki
Yamashita<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoyuki_Yamashita>,
who surrendered on 2 September 1945, in Kiangan. "It was a great moment for
the 32nd," wrote Maj. Gen. William H. Hill, commanding general of the Red
Arrow Division, "a glorious finish to this long bitter
struggle."[52]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-ramos-51>
[edit
]End of the war

Operations officially ceased on 15 August 1945 when Japan
surrendered<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan>,
although A Company beat off a banzai charge during the morning that killed
one soldier and wounded two others, and another 18 hours later in which
another soldier died and seven were wounded.

On 9 October the 32nd Division left Luzon for Japan and occupation
duty<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Japan> in
a convoy of 31 ships. They arrived on 14 October at
Sasebo<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasebo%2c_Nagasaki>,
Japan on the island of Kyushu <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu>. The
32nd stayed in Kyushu until the Division was inactivated on 28 February
1946.[18]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-remember-17>
Among
the very first to enter combat, and the very last to cease fighting, the
32nd was in combat for 654 days, more than any other unit during World War
II, and eleven of its men were awarded the Medal of
Honor<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor>
.[53]
[54]
It
was estimated the Division had killed over 32,000 Japanese soldiers during
the war.[55]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29#cite_note-54>
