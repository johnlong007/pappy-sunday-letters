Ants at Work

12 photographs·330 words

We worked up black walnuts.

Used the all purpose ATV to transport containers. Diane does the hard
part gathering and dumping into the containers. I use the tongs to pick up
ouliers

With a full load, while up on the hill behind the house, a rear tire was
ruptured. Woe, despair and misery is me. Those small diameter wheels are
tough. We have the ruptured tire off, wheel prepped and a new tire on
order, promised for tomorrow. Misery loves company.

Hulling walnuts is best done while they are still intact before starting to
dry and crack. Wear waterproof gloves, whack with rubber hammer and peel
hull off. Less than 5 seconds per nut.

Then pressure wash. Get wet head to toe.

Spread to dry then sack in one gallon bags.

We now have 61 gallons with a lot more available. We have not yet settled
on where to store to cure. Need a well ventilated, warm, dry place.

Beth hinted she might need a few more apples. I went with Sanford to his
growing up neighborhood near Burbank.

When the pool people finished their work they added too much salt. Diane
has been testing and adjusting each week since then. On the last testing
trip they said getting more swimmers will help speed the process. Perhaps
apple butter making can also be a pool party.

Some work on the A-C. We managed to install a new tube in the rear
wheel without pulling the wheel from the tractor, Anita helped. Just like
the pros.

It starts ok, runs ok but carb. is not shutting fuel off properly.Carb
is off now waiting for a competent person to open and check the float and
needle valve.

B & L took us in one night. I feel very fortunate in a lot of ways. Family,
friends, faith.
