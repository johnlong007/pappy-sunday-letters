March 3, 2013 · Sunday

On the Other Hand

13 photographs·482 words

I started the week by catching my chain saw mangled index finger
between two fifty pound iron blocks. It hurt. By the time we went to
visit Rebecca on Wed. it was very sore and I had about learned to
leave it extended to avoid hitting things. However, when turning the
steering wheel with the index finger extended it hits the wiper switch
arm. That hurts. Rebecca suggested I should try steel fingered gloves.
So, when I got home, I dug out the molded finger protector from the
previous incident. That has helped. By the end of the week it has
dropped in swelling but increased in sensitivity.

Even with a wounded warrior. clearing brush from woods continued. The
weather was bad enough that Carl cancelled AT work and we were out of
town for Friday hike so no report there. But, we did make good
progress on the brush.

In doors, work continued on the MM. All the valves are out. Dwight dug
through his parts inventory and found hardly used valves which fit
right in. He says he can find a seat grinding tool so we will be in
business there. However, the engine will not rotate. I decided that
rather than forcing it I would get a longer handle for the crank. That
did not work as I intended. Fortunately, when the bolt which the crank
rotates started to turn easily I recognized going on the downhill side
of the stress strain curve and stopped turning. So, The bolt could
still be removed and we hope and expected can be repaired by Pat the
welder.

As some people might know, one of GSH's joys in life is harassing me
about bringing car parts into the dining room/kitchen to work. She has
started a new carpentry project. Stay tuned.

On a similar note, Charlie, do you remember the suction pump we
procured and could not get to work? I dragged it out to try to suction
excessive mouse milk from the MM cylinders above the pistons. I was
ready to throw it into the bin for Wayne to send back to China when
GSH asked for a chance to work on it. Turns out the piston seal is
adjustable and it worked as advertised after her tender ,healing
touch.

Speaking of healing touch, keeping watch on Rebecca during her time of
confinement lasted one full day!

But, since that was the role for GSH, I took my saw and worked on
clearing the fence row so Dale and helpers can put up a fence. The
fence is the one perpendicular to the creek at the end of the field
away from the house. Dale had cleared most of the route in the
direction GSH is looking in the picture. I cut brush in the other
direction so there will be no shortage of work for willing workers as
opportunities arise.
