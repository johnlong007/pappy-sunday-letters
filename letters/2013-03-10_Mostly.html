March 10, 2013 · Sunday

Mostly

8 photographs·707 words

I am sure almost everyone knows we had a big event this week. Anita
became a grandmother !

In other exciting news, I finally got back to do-gooder work with
brother Dave Dunham. (For those who do not know, Appalachian culture
calls for all church family members to be "brother" or "sister") So,
even thought Dave has bailed on CHCC once more and this time landed at
first Christian in downtown Kpt. it is close enough so he can still be
"brother Dave". Anyway, we got called in for a do-gooder project at
the church. It is a fairly old building built before A/C. So, the
windows are operable. (They also leak a lot of airflow). The folks
decided they wanted new blinds installed and Dave offered to do the
job.

The old blinds were installed at the outer edge of the window recess
with fasteners screwed into the concrete. We set in to install the
replacements in the same location. the first couple of holes ate up
Dave's drill bits so Carol was dispatched to procure more bits. When
she returned she reported the clerk told her we needed a hammer drill.
I called Dwight who assured me he had at least one and would deliver
it shortly. When he arrived, he watched while Dave drilled one hole,
the bit cut right through with the hammer drill. Then, Dwight asked
Dave why are you installing out there. If you put it in next to the
window pane it could attach to the wood frame. The net result being
the first blind took four hours and the next nine took three.

The weather was pretty rough so Carl did not schedule ant AT work.
That left me to do indoor work on the MM. I could not get the engine
to break loose so I finally removed the cylinder blocks with the
pistons still installed. The unit are not in the shop being soaked in
hot solvent. Stay tuned.

Saturday a couple of big events here. Other events elsewhere.

Charlie showed up at the crack of dawn (Yes, our Charlie whom we all
know and love was here at 7 AM) in his new Miata with a gleam in his
eye and a box of parts under his arm. He installed a new timing belt
and new seals on the cams and crank. He left driving it so we hope and
expect he made it home as planned.

A number of family members intend to take the CCP course. Toward that
objective, a practice session was held here Saturday PM. All
participants were able to keep all bullets in the assigned targets at
specified ranges. For that part, it appears we are ready. But, there
seems to be quite a bit of room for improvement in handling and
loading.

In almost equally exciting news. Benjamin had an interview with a
Texas A &M alumni who controls a meaningful scholarship fund. In the
course of the interview he asked Benj if he knows what a sliderule is.
Benj told him yes because his aged grandfather was(in a previous life)
an engineer and had explained a sliderule to him. Therefore, if
anything good comes out of this we will know where at least partial
credit is due.

Sunday was the mandatory trip to Seymour. We took Paint supplies and a
new paint spray gun. When we left, Pat was happily (Benzine I think)
spraying the components. As a bonus, I took the rank drive which I had
wrung off last week (To quote Dwight "Don't strain yourself, get a
longer handle") and got Pat to weld it back for me.

Debby Kidd ( Husband Larry died recently) has an uncle who lives in
Manila. She has been in frequent contact with him during Larry's
illness/demise. She told him she had a friend who wanted to visit the
Philippines. He told her bring him and come right on I will put you up
and show you around.

Debby got quite enthused and has applied for a passport. After the
tractor meeting the other night she called and said she did not think
he was really serious and she thought it would cost too much anyway.
So, easy come, easy go.
