Two things stood out from the Morning Edition segment about the Furman
professorial protests over President Bush's proposed commencement address.

The first is the appalling narcissism of the professors involved. I realize
that college professors are accustomed to being the center of attention, but
commencement is not about them. Commencement is a time for the graduating
seniors to be center stage, and the professorate should have enough humility
to stay in the background.

The second thing I noticed was Psychology Professor Carmella Epright's
ridiculous assertion that listening to the President without protest implied
that she condoned all of his policies. Does Professor Epright always
believe that complete agreement is a precondition for listening or does this
standard only apply to the President? Does Professor Epright believe, for
example, that Barack Obama's proposal to listen to Ahmadinejad and Castro
implies agreement with all of their policies? Does Professor Epright also
believe that the mostly conservative Furman student body should only listen
to her lectures if they agree with her politics? And if a student should
disagree with Professor Epright's politics, would Professor Epright then
support that student's moral obligation to engage in juvenile protests that
disrupt her class?

Respectful, or at least silent, listening is not and never has been an
indicator of support or of agreement. Respectful listening is instead a
sign of maturity and willingness to learn. It seems that Furman students
are ahead of their professors in both regards.

--
Bob and Laura Jack
