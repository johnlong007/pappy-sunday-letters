July 27, 2014 · Sunday

Multiple Exposure

10 photographs·596 words

Pat Loven called and asked me to go with him on an AT work assignment he
had been given. The ATC had received a grant to hire a person to organize a
fight against an invasive species of vine considered to be a risk to the
AT. Oriental Bittersweet, you can read about it.

The ATC people put the young man in contact with our leader, Carl, who in
turn passed him on to Pat.

Neither of us had ever heard of the species but recognized it right off the
bat.

It is a heavy vine like wild grapevine but covers the trees like kudzu. It
grows in the trees around clearings.

​
We met the young man at Roan Mountain and went in Pat's AWD to the
designated spot via forest service roads.

He recently graduated from Warren Wilson college in Asheville.

http://www.warren-wilson.edu/work/

He is from Ohio but came to WW because one of his cousins had gone there
and loved it. He also thought it was a wonderful place. He took history but
decided he would like to be able to get a job so he got a second major in
Forestry leading to the temporary jod with the ATC where he hoped to do
such a good job that he could become a permanent employee.

He was ever so serious, had a beard, a first name pronounced "Reece" but
spelled Rhyse and a hyphenated last name (or two). Also had small,
tastefully understated earrings.

It was delight working with him. Pat and I carried on a running mutual
harassment of each other while cutting all The vines at ground level from
all the trees except one.

Rhyse, completely cleared one small tree of all traces of the vines
explaining that he had to return with pictures of his days work.

In a somewhat related vein, Dave Hrivnek came by here Saturday. When I
worked at ECC the company would send bright young IEs out into the
manufacturing areas to work. I had several assigned to my work areas over
the years All but one were very bright, energetic and capable. Dave was one
of those. As I remember he grew up in Pittsburgh and still talks funny.

Anyway, Dave has turned green to the core. While in this house we aimed for
minimum energy demands and an almost completely passive system,Dave has
gone in the other direction with active systems. He showed me a picture of
his house covered with solar panels to convert sunlight into electricity,
Over the years he has modified three vehicles ( a Ford SUV, a Jeep and a
Miata) to all electric and he is now the VERY proud owner of a Tesla.

Once more, it was delightful experience.

Back in the real world, Joe hired some good ole boys to delimb a big Oak
tree at the edge of the Blountville cemetery. Wayne promised to remove the
residue but since he was out of town GSH and I removed the part actually on
the cemetery and left the part across the drive on the Long property to be
addressed at some future date.

​

​

​

​Those who have spent time around here, know all the chains are kept on the
tractor front bumper. I left the rig parked overnight at the cemetery.

So, at the end of the week, the cemetery is clear and the usable firewood
is piled down here awaiting WW's to convert into usable form.
