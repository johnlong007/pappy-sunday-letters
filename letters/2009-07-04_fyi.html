KINGSPORT — Advanced Placement Latin lives on at Dobyns-Bennett High
School, as does Advanced Placement physics.
Kingsport City Schools Superintendent Richard Kitzmiller said the system
has hired a replacement AP Latin teacher for the retiring Sandy Hickman, and
that AP Latin will be offered at D-B this fall.
The class had been in question because of cuts the BOE made in its budget
after the Board of Mayor and Aldermen funded only $800,000 of a more than
$1.75 million funding increase request.
The AP physics class also was saved but because of lower demand will be
merged into one section instead of two, Kitzmiller said previously. An AP art
class that drew only three students will not be offered at D-B in the fall.
AP students can take a test at the end of the course and, if they score
high enough, receive college credit in that subject. Another option is
dual-enrollment courses that give high school and college credit at the same
time.
Hickman, students and parents rallied behind the Latin program during a
Kingsport Board of Education meeting earlier this year, arguing — among other
things — that it was unfair to do away with the final year of Latin for
students who had planned to take the entire series of classes and take the AP
exam.
Likewise, now-retired BOE member Pat Turner and Jefferson Elementary
fourth-grader Michael Bennett rallied behind the elementary Spanish program. But
the BOE — without Turner’s vote — last month cut the six elementary
Spanish teaching positions from the budget. Bennett earlier in the budget process
took his plea to save Spanish to the BMA.
Delores Richmond, assistant superintendent of human resources, school
nutrition services and health services, said at a BOE meeting Thursday night
that of those six teachers, one had already resigned, and three tenured ones
have found jobs — two as teachers in the Kingsport system and one as an
instructional assistant put on a first-priority list for any suitable open
position.
Of the two non-tenured teachers, Richmond said one has secured a job in the
Sullivan County system, and another is looking for a teaching job in
Texas.
Richmond said the holders of other teaching positions cut in the budget all
have found jobs.
BOE President Susan Lodal thanked Richmond and the Central Office staff for
helping find positions for displaced teachers.
