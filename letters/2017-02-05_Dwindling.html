Dwindling

8 photographs·527 words

There were several couples from our class of '54 who got married. Only a
few lasted until death did them part.

If you read these two sites , there is a connection. My father was
recruited to work in the Kaiser shipyard in Vancouver Washington in ww2.
While there, he learned the sheet metal working skill and was employed by
Liberty Lumber after he returned.

The owner, Mr. Renfro, was John Macks' father (he always went by that
name). The "tinsmith" mentioned was my father.

John Mack and Audree always came to the class reunions. Even after
splitting and with replacement spouses. And they always cut up and kidded
each other.

In related news, We visited cousin and sister-in-law Betty Buchanan again
this week. Advanced pancreatic cancer. Not looking good at all.

Cousin James Buchanan and all his family were in Erwin due to the death of
his F-I-L. We made a run over to check in with them. All kids seem well and
happy. Did not get to see Lisa.

Did stop in to see Aunt Flo while in Erwin. 89 and going strong. I
collected a bit more family information from her and took a picture of a
chair hand made by one of her uncles.

​Her house is full of family artifacts.

No AT work this week, Still trying to get my act together to go walk our
section. Maybe Tuesday this week. I spent all my available outside time
working on the '49.

Made progress on the inside and outside of the cab.

​Inside, GSH has a flower blooming. my mother would be pleased (probably
surprised also). She has been working to
get the seat for the '49 in decent shape. It may go back in this week. '63
is at a standstill until the parts ordered in July get here.

​
Saturday, Wayne came by and helped a bit with odds 'n ends including moving
the truck.

No Dr. appointments but I have one this coming week and we each have more
week after that (if I survive the race). However, we now have pills. Each
of us, morning pills and evening pills. Who would have thought it! Diane
has taken on the task of keeping them sorted for us.

Daddy bought the "lower place" in the late '40s. It was about a mile away
from the house. We walked back and forth once or twice each day for many
years when not in school. To get there, we left the road about where the
Roadside rest restaurant is now located and cut across the Davis property
usually stopping by their spring house to get water. (No water fit to drink at
our property). We also passed a cemetery (Brown) one of the oldest in upper
East Tn, One of the GGC lives on the place now. The barn is falling down.

​
I have been pondering all these grandchildren planning to get married.
Seems that life could be made simpler (for some people) if the involved
parties could all agree on some sort of combined, joint event.

Just a thought, Sort of like cheaper by the dozen.
