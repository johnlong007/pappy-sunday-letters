Boeing got contract for first stage of the JFK moon rocket in 1962.

Boeing sent a team to Huntsville to work with Werner etc on the preliminary
design for 1 year then on to NO to do detail design and fabrication.
I was a staff engineer assigned to a design group.
The mission had an overall assigned reliability. I am not sure I remember
number. Maybe 95% confidence of 95 % success. With success being no
catastrophic failure. i.e someone gets killed.

My group had the LOX and fuel tanks. Millions of pounds in each. Tanks had
to be kept at constant pressure with inert gas during entire launch and
ascent. 156 seconds as I remember to burn 6 Million pounds.

LN2 cylinders were submerged in the LOX tank.

I specified and specked out a manifold system with solenoid valves to
control internal pressure in the tanks as level went down and outside
pressure went down and rocket went up.

Werner had bad experience with pressure regulators on his German rockets so
no regulators were allowed.

Played the solenoid valves like a piano and pressure looked like a saw
blade.

Specs on the solenoid were something like 99.99995 . Sort of ran up the
cost of lab testing but never had a mission failure.

I bet Space X has regulators.
