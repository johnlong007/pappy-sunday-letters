Passing Parade

16 photographs·484 words

Graduation day, UT, august 21st. 1958

Our furniture was on the way to Seattle. We stayed with a high school
friend, June Tapp, until I dropped GSH off at the UT hospital. I moved into
our car in the parking lot until she was released. Made a trip to Erwin and
closed out our savings account. Used the money to pay hospital and with
about $100 left headed for Seattle, slept in the car.
Mom and Dad took our growing family to Erwin until I got reimbursed for
travel by Boeing, sent her the money and she flew across the country with a
two week old, a two year old.
DC 3s and DC 7s.

Here we are later, you can do your own arithmetic.

A & C put on a very fine event for family and friends.

Farmer Lisa puts a lot of ,iles on her car. Does a generally good job
getting routine maintenance but does not always get served well.. It
appears the person who last replaces the brake pads did not do a proper job
of cleaning and lubricating the sliding surfaces. Or, perhaps too much sale
had its way. Either way, Alan came to the rescue and sent her on her
way.without the noise with which she arrived.

I do not have many regrets concerning my Mom. But one is I wish I had tried
to get her to go on a motorcycle ride with me.Now, I will never know. Who
would have thought I coul pickup GSH in our own driveway while I was
driving the Bobcat.
When I was a senior in high school we had a new. 1953 Plymouth and a 1949
Dodge one ton truck. I always took the truck on a first date. Figured if
they were too good for the truck they were two good for me.

A & C procured some proper paint , PBBD finished off the dent repair
project.

We went over to check on Aunt Flo, last of Mom's sisters. I asked her about
my GGM Mary Jane Parks Howell. She died when I was four but I remember
several things ablout her. Giving me the wooden egg and watching her card
and spin wool. She showed me the cabinet in which MJP kept her food. The
other cabinet has a different story. Flo snabbled almost all the family
artifacts

Rachel and Andrew quizzed me about chestnuts and I told them about
chinquapins. Benj will recognize, maybe others? Looks like a big crop but
very poor chestnut crop.

Before the BD event I walked around the original trac we purchased in
1966. I am beginning to understand what old men do when they sit around by
themselves.
