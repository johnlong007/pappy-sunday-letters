January 22, 2012 · Sunday

Great Grand Pa !

8 photographs·651 words

No, not me, Herb.

Cathy's daughter Allison is married to an ECC engineer. This week they became the parents (proud I would suppose) of a daughter. If I heard and remember correctly named Callie.

On a less festive note, Herb's daughter Pam is in the hospital in JC. We went to see her today Seems to be doing OK but I have heard no diagnosis or prognosis.

While we are on family news, weather and sports, some of us went to Union City to visit my spritely sister and help with her most recent birthday celebration.

FYI, at Christmas, the Union City churches put together a video giving the history of the "older" churches in town. Sis had a starring role with walking, talking visit through the !st. Baptist Church. Sherrill ,I am sure, was watching and filled with pride (actually, I don't guess one is allowed pride in heaven). Anyway, she did an excellent narration. I confiscated her CD and will make it available for loan upon request.

Meanwhile, here in River City, do-gooder work continued. GSH and I went by Sue Ketrons to get information about Hal's tractor. She asked me to sell it for her. I copied the serial number and took pictures. It is a 1949 Ford 8N. Implements are a scoop, a single bottom plow, boom pole, single row cultivator and a disc. If you know anyone who is interested the whole lot for $2,200. I think it is worth more than that but that is her price.
Also, Dave and I visited a newly displaced wife (whose husband is currently deployed in a far away place with a strange sounding name) to do some carpentry repair work.

In a fit of kindness, GSH and I offered to go grocery shopping for the unnamed displaced wife. Clearly she is not from around here as the list did NOT include soup beans or corn meal. Work to be done there.

Speaking of GSH , she more or less offered to help me move the next Winter project out of storage and into the shop.

You may not be able to tell but she is standing on the running board steering while I was on the tractor dragging the truck backwards up and around the incinerator to the drive. Fortunately, while one is on the tractor, all voices are drowned out.

The truck, a 1937 Plymouth is now in the shop. A truck like this is the one my father bought new when I was less than one. He kept it until 1949 and traded it off for one like the one ton Dodge which is in the storage building. I bought it from Hemmings motor news in the early '70s. Some may remember going to Fairboult MN. to get it. We towed it back with the little blue van.

What else happened? AT work was Friday. I missed it to go visit Sis. Oh, I know ! We unloaded the car.

Turned out to be a bit more than I expected. When I started to move the truck to position it to unload car, I found the batteries were dead. Real dead. Seems the interior light in the trailer had been left on. When I tried to drag the whole rig with the tractor, the tractor ran out of fuel. The gage is not accurate!

Anyway, the obstacles were overcome and the car is unloaded. I think I will leave it on display for a couple of weeks before putting into storage for some indefinite period of time.

I did procure a new tire for the Bob Cat. Finally got fed up with the plugged tire and the slow leak. Also finished trimming the trunk of the big Oak tree. Now, I hope to get someone to come examine it for potential value before it becomes next Winters firewood.
