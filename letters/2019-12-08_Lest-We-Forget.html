December 8, 2019 · Sunday

Lest We Forget

12 photographs·457 words

My uncle Jim graduated from UCHS and volunteered for service. Being a high
school graduate and a volunteer he got to chose his branch and went into
the Army Air Corps.
After basic he was sent to Hawaii along with a shipment of dismantled
B-17s. Later, he was sent on to Clark field along with the B-17s to put
them into service.
The Japanese hit Clark 7 hours after Hickam.
He told me about being out on the runway, under a truck shooting at the Japs
with his 45. He could see them looking out and laughing while strafing.
He went in as a private and came out in '45 as a captain (but with TB). In
the picture above he is the one with sunglasses picture taken at Letterman
hospital which he passed through on his way home.
Another uncle, Ed, was drafter later and fought the entire war as part of
MacArthur's Island hopping.
I feel privileged to have spent a good amount of time with Jim and much
more with Ed. Each a real man.

This week as been very average (another story).

A lot of time spent fiddling with the grill for the '37. Current status but
miles to go.

Alan under the watchful (admiring?) eye of his mother refurbished her clock.

The clock was a kit which I procured for her Christmas present about 1975
or so. Alan assembled it as his part of the present.

Now, as of this afternoon, it is ticking along.

For church Wed night, the "adults" constructed gingerbread houses. This
coming Wed. night, the children are to decorate. We went down where I
watched the events and GSH wandered around offering constructive advice.
Look close.

Speaking of excitement, Thursday was free hot fudge cake at all Shoney's.
It fir right in as I had appointment with Dr. Sago at 1PM. (another
cutaneous squamous carcinoma). We met Sanford for lunch. Partly free.

The big event for the week was taking the '63 to get a new top installed. I
told the installer I want it just like original.

Car was delivered on Dec. 6 , 1962 I believe. Not long before Laura was
delivered.

This picture is of an old Singer sewing machine very much like the one my
mother used. It was also a GSH Christmas present and has been restored to
proper function .

If anyone is falling in love with GSH. I have about learned my lesson.

For those interested, the water retention basin seems to have worked in
first test. Remember the picture last week? Almost all dissipated.
