August 9, 2020 · Sunday

Potpourri

12 photographs·429 words

I have read that ones meals are indicative of their socio-economis station in
life. At the bottom of the ladder quantity is the important issue. For the
middle , blue collar level Quality is the most important factor. For the
top echelon ,presentation.
I look at our lunches and think about where that pegs us.

While we were out admiring grandson sprout wings of an Eagle, willing
workers descended upon our house. Now, we have a downstairs "bedroom".
Before Laura presented us with our first cat she told me a cat will always
find the most comfortable place in the house to sleep. So, I guess the new
bed ranks first.
It will also serve as a guest bedroom and unlike the rooms down the hall is
in a climate controlled environment.

At my request, Wayne fabricated and installed a section of handrail . Here,
GSH is doing her Scarlet O'Hara impersonation.

It was raining one day so I decided to spend a couple of hours installed
hood and grill trim on the '37.
I set a new indoor record for making a simple job difficult.
While fiddling with the '37 I also moved the temporary fuel tank a bit in
order to be able to install hood over the engine compartment. I intend to
get it out and run up and down drive soon.

On the gardening scene, the little pointy peppers which survived
overwintering in spite of me are alive and well.

Neither alive nor well, we have another dead pine tree. This one is bugging
our neighbor Larry and I promised to cut it down. Now it is bugging me .
Moving up on the to do list. Cutting it down will be simple. Getting rid of
the log and limbs not so simple or easy.

On our Sunday afternoon tour of the Kingdom we came across a downed Maple
tree above the upper pond. It was hollow all the way up to the limbs. I
intend to saw it up and see how what I can salvage. No good reason, just
curiosity.

Red Bud trees are pretty about one week per year. The rest of the time they
are a pain. This one split and fell .

Sawed trunk into firewood and dragged brush to brush pond. Always something.

Can anyone recognize this tread pattern? If so, tell the owner and/or
operator that grass on a hill is slippery when wet.
