January 13, 2013 · Sunday

Good News

8 photographs·559 words

I got a report from Vanessa that all is well. I got a report from GSH
attesting that Beth is well. Along the way, various and sundry
Children and grandchildren checked in with reports of life hither and
yon. In addition to all that, I check Facebook everyday to see what is
being posted.The information age is truly remarkable and (mostly) I
like it.

Meanwhile, in River City I decided it was time to have a go at getting
the '37 running. Up until now, the windshield was just stuck in place
without being attached. But, it is now installed in at least a
semi-permanent manner.The ignition system has been checked and is
delivering a nice hot spark. Dwight stopped by helping to confirm that
the nice hot spark is being delivered at the correct time and to the
correct places.
Dave Edwards,traveled across the mountains for the stated purpose of
wishing me "Happy Birthday". I would like to note that Dave and Pat
had a surprise birthday event for me 27 years ago. Wow, time does fly
by.
So, with Dwight and Dave watching, assisting and commenting, we tried
to start the '37. It did not start, it never hit a lick.Dwight
pronounced the failure due to low compression. The cure being and
engine overhaul. I am not yet ready to accept that prescription.

For Christmas, GSH remembered that some time back I had made a wishful
comment about L.L. Bean last forever waterproof rubber/leather boots
and arranges for Santa to deliver said boots. This week, I explained
to GSH that the boots are not hiking boots and could be damaged easily
by an errant mattock swing so I intended to be quite careful to make
sure they lasted as long or longer than I do. For AT work this week I
was expecting a long walk on a dry trail and chose not to wear my new,
waterproof boots.
Guess what, I was assigned a project to renovate a water supply which
only involved maybe a 1/2 mile walk and a full day digging in muck.

As most know, Herb has contacts inside the UCHS vocational school
including the greenhouse. He delivered some tomatoes which actually
look, feel and taste like tomatoes.

We have put 100,000 miles on the VW so I decided it was time to check
the brake pads. The front were ok but the rear ones only had 5-10
miles left so I decided to replace them.
brake pads are simple, take off the caliper, extract the old pads,
compress the piston, slide in the new pads, replace caliper and drive
away.

Except on a VW. The piston would not compress. However, I hope my
mechanic helpers will remember the admonition that if you are having
to force it you are probably doing it wrong. So, back to Advance,
borrowed the piston compressor and after a lot of fumbling discovered
that the piston rotates to retract. I did not need the special tool at
all just the trick.

Sunday, Wayne came by to loan me a photo scanner . At least that is
what he said. Actually, it turns out he was also looking for a place
to park the Chrysler while he devotes his full (discretionary) time to
getting the new and improved sedan into a higher state (Thermodynamics
students will recognize that).
