*Memorial Day* is a US federal
holiday<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_holidays_in_the_United_States>
wherein
the men and women who died while serving in the United States Armed
Forces<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces> are
remembered.[1] <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day#cite_note-1> The
holiday, which is celebrated every year on the final Monday of
May,[2]<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day#cite_note-2> was
formerly known as *Decoration Day*and originated after the American Civil
War <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War> to commemorate the
Union <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War)> and
Confederate <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America>
soldiers
who died in the Civil War. By the 20th century, Memorial Day had been
extended to honor all Americans who have died while in the military
service. It typically marks the start of the summer vacation season,
while Labor
Day <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day> marks its end.

Many people visit cemeteries and memorials, particularly to honor those who
have died in military service. Many volunteers place an American flag on
each grave in national
cemeteries<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_cemetery>
.

Annual Decoration Days for particular cemeteries are held on a Sunday in
late spring or early summer in some rural areas of the American South,
notably in the mountains. In cases involving a family graveyard where
remote ancestors as well as those who were deceased more recently are
buried, this may take on the character of an extended family reunion to
which some people travel hundreds of miles. People gather on the designated
day and put flowers on graves and renew contacts with kinfolk and others.
There often is a religious service and a "dinner on the ground," the
traditional term for a potluck <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potluck> meal
in which people used to spread the dishes out on sheets or tablecloths on
the grass. It is believed that this practice began before the American
Civil War and thus may reflect the real origin of the "memorial day" idea.
[3] <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day#cite_note-3>

Memorial Day is not to be confused with Veterans
Day<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Day>;
Memorial Day is a day of remembering the men and women who died while
serving, while Veterans Day celebrates the service of all U.S. military
veterans.[4] <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day#cite_note-4>
