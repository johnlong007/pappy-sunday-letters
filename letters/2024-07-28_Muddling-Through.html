July 28, 2024 · Sunday

Muddling Through

12 photographs·389 words

One handle on the spike tooth harrow was not usable. After some difficulty,
we found a piece of wood which looked ok and a sawmill operator willing to
convert it into rough cut handle blanks. One is now in process and seven
are in storage. Stay tuned..

Mike, the mountain man, said the log we used for the handles is Elm. I was
able to saw the leftover part into firewood length. It is the toughest log
splitting I have ever tried. Fortunately I have a first class WW. Tried to
enlist some others to no avail.

The lawn mower is back in operation thanks to the family handyman.

The spike tooth harrow has four sections containing spikes, Each spike is
hand made with a perfect hole, hand made, not too tight to split the wood,
not too loose to fall out. Bob is supplying sharp chisels to help make it
all work out.

Blacksmith Charlotte was by today and seemed interested and I think will be
willing to make replacement spikes if things go wrong.i

A lot of thought and work on the circle this week. For my part, I delivered
4 Bobcat buckets of gravel to build around the center. After 2 trips to the
repair shop and a lot of time and money, the Bobcat is leaking hydraulic
fluid again. Note 4 trails.

Management arrived to inspect progress so far.

When I went to Temple Hill school there was no school lunch. Bring your own
in a brown poke. I always had peanut butter and crackers. Sometimes with
raisins. Sometimes, in season, I had a tomato. I am not sure which is my
favorite meal That or soupbeans and cornbread.

I told Meredith, grandchildren are more fun than children and GGC may be
the most fun of all !

None of this crowd looks joyous but I thought it was a delightful visit. I
pretty well got caught up on their lives, at least the parts they were
willing to share., and both listened while I dispensed wisdom and guidance.
Buy low, sell high.

Hard to pick out but two mother turkey hens and several frying size
turklets were wandering through.
