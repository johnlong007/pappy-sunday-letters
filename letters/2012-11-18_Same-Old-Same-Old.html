November 18, 2012 · Sunday

Same Old Same Old

11 photographs·656 words

My visiting this week was mostly to see Larry Kidd's wife Debby at
HVCH. He has been in a coma for four weeks today in the CCU. Thursday
he fluttered his eyes and responded to voices for the first time. No
family was there with Debby today so I talked at length. I advised her
to talk with the hospital office about how charges are being
accumulated and to contact Larry's insurance carrier to alert them and
get familiar with the latest medicare rules. She is already thinking
about long term care of a disable person.

What I have relearned out of this situation is:
1. There is no guarantee of tomorrow
2. Make sure your paperwork concerning power of attourney, living
will, etc is current.

AT work was continued renovating the trail between Indian Grave Gap
and Chestoa. With time, the trail sloughs off on the upper side and
the hikers then move to the downhill side. I will include a picture
which shows what happens. The trail was roots all the way for this
outing making for very tough digging. I was working with a young guy,
maybe 35-40. He made the roots and dirt fly all day long. I did a
while then have to take a rest. Fortunately, each person sets their
own pace.
After we finish a section we normally sprinkle leaves on the bare
ground to prevent being slick and muddy after a rain.

You may remember the big Oak tree we finally got cut, sawed and split.
I left quite a bit in sawed to firewood lengths to be split later.
But, all that was split I loaded on the trailer quite some time back.
I do not like to leave a load on wheeled vehicles for fear of flat
spotting the bearing so, we finally got around to unloading the
trailer and stacking the wood. Unless this year is unusually cold this
stack will be for next Winter.

Brian Paley is a Canadian who moved to the US many years back. I am
not sure but think he worked for IBM in the Atlanta area. When he
retired three years back he found a lake lot on a Watuga Lake inlet
near old (underwater) Butler and had a timber frame house built on the
lot.
Brian works with us on the AT but mostly when we are trail clearing,
not routine work. Anyway, I have kept up with his house reports and
really wanted to see the finished product.

Last Friday, the Friday hikers scheduled their hike on National Forest
land across the inlet from Brian's house to be followed by a chili
lunch at the new house.GSH and I went.

The hike was very interesting, it went through the stone fences and
foundations of various houses and outbuilding as we worked our way up
a Hollow. Then, we climbed up to the top of the ridge over looking the
inlet and walked back to the car park.

The Tennessee Conservationist for July/August has an article about
lichens. I had just finished reading it before the hike so I checked a
lot of lichens along the way. I sort of vaguely remembered for an
Alaska nature talk that lichen get their nourishment from the
atmosphere and take nothing from the host (also that raindeer eat
lichen). Aaron might like to know that lichen can also be an emergency
food (but some are poisonous).

After having visited David's new house a couple of weeks back I was
interested to see how Brian's compares. It is about twice as big and
twice as fancy. Still the same design, a basement, a first floor and
1/2 loft.

On a more mundane level, GSH continued to work on her project and I
continued to piddle with the '37. So far, the reports from the TV
repair man are not encouraging.

I am about ready to start thinking about a trip to somewhere.
