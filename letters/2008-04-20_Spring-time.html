April 20, 2008 · Sunday

Spring time !

10 photographs·413 words

This week included hiking, do-gooder and AT work along with visiting the
halt, lame and afflicted. In addition, a good friend and peer from my previous
life at ECC dropped off w/o warning. Enough to make me doubly appreciate every
day and resolve not to waste a minute (by being unhappy).

As Diane noted, the rafters are installed on the outhouse. Not sure if we
are going to put the tin roof on or leave that to the pros. Dwight may have
enjoyed all the amateur work he can stand.

If you know where to look and look close, you should be able to spot both
Allison and Meredith in the band picture (about middle of the front row).

My able assistant was testing the plows for me. I wanted to plow a strip in
the front (old soccer field) for this years tomato patch. Trying to get away
from the blight. Plows would not go in it was so hard. I provided weight on
the plows while she plowed the old garden. I switched to the drag plows for the
front field. The "new) soccer field is seeded but no sign of grass so far.
It will, we hope, come on in time for camp.

Herb has long since surged into the lead in dead in dying vehicles. But, to
cinch his position, he and I dragged another one in. I included this picture
for two reasons 1. to prove the depths to witch a dead car collector will sink
and 2. to show the lowest slung britches I have ever seen. He must have them
pinned to his body. One of the vocational school students helping us get on
the road.

Having given up the competition on dead cars, I now only bring home
treasures, pearls of great worth, my helpers , of course, rushed out to admire my
latest.

The drag plows really sink in, I like them as can throw dirt uphill and plow
a curve. But, they require about 1/2 A. to make a turn.

This morning, four deer were having breakfast out by the garden. This one
from an apple tree.

The turn around is looking the best ever, thanks to Summer Camp workers.

Can all identify the bird species?

Note the cherry tree. I think we are safe from frost this year. I can almost
taste the pies.
