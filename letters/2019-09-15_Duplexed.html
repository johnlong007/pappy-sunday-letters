Fall is a lovely time of year in East Tn. Wonder how things look in the
far reaches of the family domain? I bet a lot of interesting things are
going on. Gwendolyn is in Portland and Aaron a long way in the other
direction. Kenneth and Emily are already into tomorrow. Others somewhere in
between.

Mean while, back on the ranch, it was a strange week.

The major objective was mowing the "hillside" after a couple of years of
neglect. After a fair amount of adjusting and a few warm up passes, it all
came back and the sickle mower operated perfectly for the job.

Wayne has pretty much convinced me the starter in the '63 should be
replaced. Around 1960, Chrysler switched from direct drive starter motors
to a gear reduction unit. However, according to local expert, any will fit
ant big block Chrysler .
So I went into inventory and pulled out a selection of starters.

Installing a starter should be easy. It was not.

Eventually, after many fits ,starts and skinned hands and arms, I
determined the expert was wrong. (Once more John).
The big block with a manual transmission will only accept the old, direct
drive starter. Even then, it will not fit just any, only a certain one.

Wayne is going to take the original starter to get rebuilt this week. Stay
tuned.

Saturday was flea market day in downtown Blountville. We ate lunch there.
Guess what we ate.

Wayne wants to master welding. I have all kinds (Stick AC/DC, MIG and TIG
units). None has been use in a long while. Wayne was using the MIG unit but
had a lot of trouble getting the wire to feed smoothly. Like most
equipment, it functions better if used often.

GSH and I walked up on the hill while he was welding. Pears and chestnuts
are almost ready.

Bob Harvey, Collins and Janice came over one day to check for Paw Paws.
Harvested some but crop is pretty sparse.
