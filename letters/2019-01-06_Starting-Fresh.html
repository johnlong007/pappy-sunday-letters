January 6, 2019 · Sunday

Starting Fresh

11 photographs·721 words

I think the New Year is a good time to look back and plan ahead.

I think I still have the list I made in 1958 while I was (at work) with
Boeing in Seattle. It did not get updated until time to retire from ECC
when I made a ten year plan, then a 5 year plan, then a 3 year plan.
Now it is one year at a time.
Last year I did not achieve my "run a marathon". It is being rolled over. I
believe I could do it in about 6 hours. Perhaps I need to hire a grandchild
to run laps with me. 105 I think. But, would prefer to run on a road course.
Driving one more lap around the US is still on the list. That one would
include visiting friends and family from far and wide.

Driving this year is reduced from our historic averages. 35,000 miles.
In spite of the market crashing and burning we broke even for the year.

I suppose my most important effort was allowing my teeth to be used in the
interest of Allison. That has been rewarding for me. We hope for her also.
Getting to participate in three Grandchildren weddings has also been most
rewarding.
Three new GGC were added to the family. Glad they were not the same as the
weddings.

The pool room project is moving, slowly. We are monitoring and recording
temperature inside and out. It is not staying as high above outside
temperature as I was expecting. It looks like the massive back wall is
staying close to deep earth temperature.

Enough of that.

We are shut down on AT work due to the government shutdown. If we work we
will not get paid. Oh, we don't get paid anyway. Oh well.

Does everyone know I let a walnut tree grow just inside the barn? Over the
years it shoved a section of the roof out. Since the roof is to be replaced
soon it seemed a good time to remove the interference. In spite of one un-named
person agitating to remove the entire tree, I decided to leave that for the
future and commissioned a younger sawyer to remove the offending portion.

As you can see, people came in from miles away to stand in awe. Or maybe to
just stand.

After the audience left, GSH lept into action.

We own a 30 foot strip of land between us and a neighbor. He came over one
day and said a portion of the big tree fell during the recent snow and had
just missed his house.
Enlisting the first willing worker I could, we dragged the downed trunk out
of his yard and into out 30 foot strip.

The limb had sprouted off the main trunk very close to ground level. I had
never noticed it at all. The notable part of this picture is the saw. I am
using a 16" saw. Before starting I sharpened the chain. I was able to saw
through on one side and go to other side and saw down through the same slot
without curvature. A historic first.

We did a fair amount on the '63. This picture is installing the jump seat.
When Laura was born I took the seat out and fabricated a wooded ark which
fit right in between the seats. It served as our portable car bed and
later parked on engine compartment in LBT for Laura, Linda and Wayne.
Any of the older ones remember it? I still have the ark overhead in the
shop in case anyone needs it.

Passenger seat sans seat back is installed.
The car was purchased with very few extras including a clock. It has a
place in the panel for a clock. So, I installed and oil pressure gage there
to supplement the idiot light.

The car has a chrome moulding underneath door on each side. I had cut out
the rusty metal and replaced so the mounting holes for the moulding clips
were gone. New clips have been obtained the installation was started. But
not finished.

Of course, we had to replenish the wood supply. One week at a time.
