November 8, 2009 · Sunday

Muddling

8 photographs·481 words

First the good news. I can fall out of the barn and still get up and walk
away.

Working on the car project we are need of a radiator. Since I have quite a
selection stored in the barn, I went to the barn to hunt. Mostly they are
stored on the third level of tier poles. Out of difference to my advanced
age and infirmities, I used a ladder (the one I was hunting for a while back,
it was in the barn right where I had left it) to climb up. But, I made the
mistake of attempting to walk along a tier pole. It broke.

I went down and checked on Hal one day. He is tired and gets discouraged
but all things considered, doing very well. Sue and the kids take him to the
antique stores and the flea markets where he continues to add to his
collecting of old toy cars and truck. Little miniature things.

My do-gooder buddy, Dave Dunham, is unhappy with CHCC and is leaving. I
think it is mainly C of C and we are too liberal for him (women lead singing
and pray in public). Not sure what I will do with Mondays.

Tuesday are still OK. We went way up past Elk Park to the over mountain
shelter to dig a relocation around a PUD (pointless up and down). The
relocated trail is going right through a ramp field. I collected a sackful. The
ones I have planted here in the woods come back each year but do not seem to
be multiplying. So, I planted these in a row in the lower garden. If they
come up in the spring, I will pull and eat. Notice the black dirt. I always
think how my Mother would have loved to have such dirt in her garden.

Work on the Studebaker is proceeding nicely. I temporarily stuck the
fenders and hood on (with bungee cords) in order to get dimensions for the
radiator.

Dwight has been rounding up parts, Installing parts and supervising me.
This week, he hooked up the clutch and brake lines to the front wheels. The
rear brakes are going to have to be overhauled and the lines connected.

Another big event for the week was fabricating a cover for the chimney. I
have given up on the contractor coming back and decided to do it myself. The
evidence is mounting that a lot ,perhaps most, of the leaks were caused by
a nail through the flashing in the upper valley and leaks in the mortar on
the chimney.

Both the cover and the bird cage are just perched and remain to be
attached.

Moths got in to the treasure stored up here. They ate holes in a sweater
which was treasured by GSH. She has worked at least 40 hours on patching the
holes. To be continued.
