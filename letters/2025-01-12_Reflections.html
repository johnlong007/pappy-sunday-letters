Reflections

13 photographs·425 words

Our clan continues to multiply and divide. I am appreciative that I have
been able to hang in and read about the additions and see pictures.

Labs this week were a bit encouraging . Looks like a good chance I will
have another birthday !

My GGM ,Mary Jane Parks Howell, her daughters, Nancy and Hanna.

Mary Jane owned most of the property up the valley where I grew up. My
father purchased the land for our homesite from MJ and later the mountain
land in front of the house from Hanna.( I purchased the mountain land from
my parents in 1965). Although MJ died when I was four, I remember her
giving me my wooden egg and I remember her funeral.

About 1946, One of Hanna's sons Rowe logged the right side of the property
. My first encounter with logging was in pouring coal(lamp) oil on a moving
cross cut saw. After cutting the trees they were sawed to length then
ballhooted down to the trail to the loading landing. Once on the trail,
the log would be hooked to a horse in harness using a logging single tree.
Trace chains came all the way from the hames on the horses collar along the
horses sides and attached to the ends of the single tree. The grab hooks
were driven into the log for dragging. The swivel allowed log to rotate
without twisting the single tree.

Note, dragging the logs out created a great bobsled run for later years
during snowfall. Another story about homemade sleds.

Pictured here is the remains of a homemade layoff plow. Pondering trying to
refurbish or just hang on display as is. I intend to consult Bob before
deciding.

All our children should remember the cookie jar. Aunt G gave it to us when
we got married in 1955. Over the years, all 8 members of the family broke
it at least once.'

It is listed here.

Letting Herbs stove set out in the weather was a serious, unforced error. A
lot of rust to contend with and a panel is missing. I sent out an APB to
movers about the panel.

How to make a silk purse.
