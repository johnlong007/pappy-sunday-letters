October 18, 2020 · Sunday

New Record

13 photographs·379 words

Each week I seem to set a new record for making simple jobs difficult.

Last week we collected walnuts from one select tree. Black walnuts have a
"hull" , a shell and a kernel. Toe prefer for human consumption, the hull
is removed and the walnut shell is allowed to air dry for 3 or 4 months.
The time tested method for removing the hull is to spread the walnuts out
on a soft surface (grass) and drive over them for a few days.

In order to speed the process, I dumped the walnuts in the truck bed and
attacked with the high pressure washer with truck parked on the ramp. It
took two days and made a world class mess.

At least the nuts are now spread out to begin curing.

Does it count as do-gooder work if it is all in the family? Monday morning,
we helped Wayne load his truck with Sarah demolition materials.

Breakfast on the work site.

Saturday, we went to Erwin and did a few things for Niece (farmer) Lisa.
Herb had started a brush pile in the middle of the hay field. Over the
years it has expanded and taken on a life of its own. She plans to burn it
or get the fire dept. to come burn. Either way I wanted to reduce some of
the spread and make it more compact.

Herb had a fine collection of heavy duty trailers. I am considering one to
be used as a base for a sawmill.

Today we harvested more pears. I hope Laura wanted pears as I delivered to
her tonight.

Wayne and Thomas harvested chestnuts today. The deer have been harvesting
about every evening, I think there are 5 left. Had seven for quite a while.

On Wed. Joe and Anita had company and invited us over for lunch. We went.

When we got home from Erwin yesterday we found a cooler of foodstuffs by
the door. Turns out Bob Harvey and Jan Mather had been out on a mission of
mercy.
We had a better than average Sunday dinner today. Friends are important.
