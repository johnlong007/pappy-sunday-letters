October 4, 2009 · Sunday

Much Better

9 photographs·521 words

I think the Studebaker project should come ahead of health, wealth and the
search for meaning.

Bought a '57 Chrysler New Yorker in 1967. The engine is now in the
Studebaker. It did pull a brief stint in the Dodge pickup about 20 years back. The
car body is buried on a farm near Bluff City. Another story for another
time.

I purchased the Studebaker in '67 also. I had a plan all along it is just
now falling into place. I worked with a fellow, Walter Hurd , who worked on
wrecked cars. I got him to give me a set of coil springs to put under the
car. This week, the springs were installed. It took a bit of heating and
beating but they are in and look OK.
I also fabricated and installed a cross member to support the transmission.
No picture but it does look a lot like a silk purse.The channel piece came
from an old binder the balance of which Charlie sent to China and is
probably now back as a Toyota.

We checked on Hal a couple of times. He had a port installed it was not an
easy process and still looks a inflamed. He will go in tomorrow for first
chemo which he expected to be repeated each three weeks. He is feeling ok
and remains upbeat

How about critters?

The groundhog was eating the tomatoes. He has now gone off for a family
reunion. The chestnuts are falling on the ground. I went out at dusk one
evening a counted five deer under the trees. There may have been more. Turkeys
have been grouping up. There a small groups around about all the time. They
make the rounds of the garden, the tomatoes and the persimmons. Anyone like
to harvest a big gobbler for Thanksgiving?

That reminds me, I told Genevieve (high school class mate and apple lady) I
would come get the apples a day or the before Thanksgiving. I believe we
should plan to peel apples Friday night, make the run on Saturday. Sis,
perhaps you and Betty can make it to Linda's and hitch a ride on up. Anyway,
if you want me to come get you I will and, if I do, you can be sure I will
take you home.

Speaking of classmates, we had out high school class reunion Friday in
conjunction with the apple festival. It was very good. After the first few
reunions everyone gets a lot more relaxed. However, out of a class of about
140, we have had 14 die during the last five years.

Blountville was the site of a flea market sale Saturday. Lot's of people.

D & D were going to Corbin today to help Pat with his dragster on a 1/4
mile strip. I did not go and have not heard a report.

We worked on the AT Tuesday. For the first time in a long spell, I carried
a chain saw , hiked for miles and sawed blow downs. It went fine, slow but
fine (like my running). Life is returning to normal, wherever that is.
