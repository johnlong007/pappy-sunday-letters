September 13, 2015 · Sunday

Full Week

13 photographs·595 words

With Grandchildren scattered hither and yon ,many going to football games,
some with active parts, it would seem one should be able to catch a glimpse
onTV. But, I did not. Did I miss something/someone.

Monday, after much preparation, I loaded the rig and set out to mow the
hillside, I took a before picture, Unfortunately, from the spot I picked,
it looks just like the after picture. I got the flat part beside thouse and
the flat part below the old pond. Then I went up the hill alongside the
(dead) Yellow pines and mowed the hill top. On down the road and down over
the hill into the back part, Made one pass there and hit a root. Mower
started making a terrible racket so shut down and brought rig home. I had
bent the stump jumper. Spent all day Tuesday working on the mower and have
it ready to try again. Stay tuned.

Last year, Pat Loven and I went with a Summer intern employed by the ATC to
do battle with and invasive species (Oriental Bittersweet) on the AT. This
year, we were called to go back to the same place for another round. This
time a couple of forest service people brought a mower in and mowed the
field while we were working. The picture shows a lot of the dead vines from
last years efforts. This year, the intern painted the stub with herbicide
when we snipped the vines. His hope is springing more eternally than mine
is. My experience fighting mother nature is not good.

Some years back, a state employed forest service person, Danny Osbourne,
came out to the hillside and made an assessment and management plan for the
hillside. At mu request, he came here and looked this place over.
He was well pleased with this place, said we have a fair amount of Oak and
Poplar ready to harvest. Recommended thinning the pines some more and
planting trees on the part out front. He pointed out we also have an
invasive species which requires snipping and painting stub. Tree of Heaven.
Stinks like Sumac but has a leaf with a little spur on underside.

Danny side it is time we had the hillside timbered and that Poplar is
selling high now.

Various fruits and nuts are coming in now. Katy might be interested in the
Persimmons. A bountiful crop. Saw three dear under the trees early this
morning. Banj liked the chinquapins. The turkeys do also. Gwendolyn likes
chestnuts, a fine crop ripening. Bob Harvey came by to get PawPaws.

I went with Herb to see Dr. Sago. She admired his wound and lamented not
having attacked the other side more vigorously early on. She than zapped a
few places for me, Asked if I had any since she had a full Dewar and
proceeded to zap several on my arms and back.

The interesting part was when she started telling about her parent teacher
conference with Cathy Moffit. Lots of hand waving and exclamations. Her 11
year old daughter is resisting Algebra.

Saturday was the Blountville flea market. Weather was not too good but the
Ruritan seemed to be doing well with soup beans and cornbread. Herb had
sent a sack full of really hot peppers but they must have decided to be
caytious.Laura has a Mexican friend who will be glad to take the peppers I
understand.

A man and wife who own Wheels of Yesteryear in Myrtle Beach SC came by
Sunday. He wants to buy the "300" I restored. Any comments?
