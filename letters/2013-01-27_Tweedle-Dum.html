January 27, 2013 · Sunday

Tweedle Dum

6 photographs·404 words

It has been a cold week in Blountville. I suppose that will bring a
hearty chuckle from the Wisconsin contingent and outright laughter
from Alaska but still felt cold to me. So, I pretty much stayed
indoors all week. I did get out for my run (shuffle) every day except
the icy one.

We were gone to visit Sis last weekend. It was cold while we were gone
and after we got back. With no heat at all in the house while we were
gone, it was 57 in my corner when we came in Monday night. I kept a
fire in fireplace and in the shop most all week. This afternoon about
4 PM it reached 70 for about five minutes. Mostly we run from about 62
in early morning to 67 in the evening. Not bad. If we do build another
house for the old folks, I want it to be designed to stay above
freezing inside without having to leave heaters running.

The high point of the week was when Herb resupplied us with tomatoes.
I have about got the BLTC sandwich worked out to both art and science.

Working on the '37 I have been scraping paint and patching tears and
dings. The truck was black when I obtained it (Fairboult, Minn. in
about 1972 as I remember). Under the black it has a heavy coat of
white primer, under the primer is what I believe is the original blue.
Sure wish I had some of my Summer Camp paint scrappers. I plan to get
all the body work done , put on a coat of primer and leave it until
Summer to paint (blue).

Saturday I went with Dwight to go check on the drag car. It is coming
right along. The big air scoop is intended to direct air to the carb.
A big Holley which has maybe a six inch opening and pulls in maybe 800
cfm. I think the scope is about 10 times too big and will slow him by
a significant amount. Anyone know a good ME who could run the
calculations?

When we started to leave ,Pat put two big boxes of nuts, bolts and
misc. hardware in the car for me. I dumped it out and wished I had
Meredith to come sort. What on earth am I going to do with all this
stuff?

No, AT, no do gooder, no adventures.
