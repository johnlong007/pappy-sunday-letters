On Sat, Apr 27, 2013 at 11:00 AM, Kim Peters wrote:

> Phyllis and hiked yesterday from 19E to the top of Hump Mountain and back.
> We had an adventure. Along the way we ran into the fellow who stopped to
> ask us about ramps on Thursday (Buckeye Cornelius). It turns out he is
> keeping a journal on trailjournals.com. I thought you might want to see
> what he wrote about the experience.
>
> Thursday, April 25
> "While walking the trail, I came across three folks working on the side of
> the hill. They appeared to be harvesting some type of plant. They said they
> were picking ramps. A ramp is a plant that looks like an onion and tastes
> like a cross between an onion and garlic. They showed me what they look
> like, how to get it from the ground and how to peel back the outer layers
> with the dirt to easily eat them as I pulled it from the ground. We talk a
> little after returning to the trail. Nice folks."
>
> Friday, April 26
> "Later, just after the last bald, I saw two ladies walking Southbound. I
> exchanged pleasantries with the first. As I approached the second lady, she
> looked familiar but I could not place her. She asked me how I liked the
> ramps yesterday. This was two of the three people that taught me how to
> pick ramps. That was over twenty miles ago. How funny. I assume she could
> easily recognize my because I had on the same clothes (I have no others)
> and the same Boonie Hat. We talked for awhile. I found that she did not
> have a trail name. She does now, if she wants to keep it. Her new trail
> name is Ramp."
>
> If you want to read the entire journal you can look here:
> http://trailjournals.com/entry.cfm?id=410124
>
> The real adventure happened when we got near the top of Hump. We
> encountered a young couple, she was carrying two packs and he was crawling
> down the trail. Apparently he had a torn achilles tendon and could no
> longer walk. They were attempting to make it to 19E, some five miles away.
> Of course, Phyllis and I knew that this would be nearly impossible for
> them and tried to figure out some way to help. We ended up calling 911 and
> Avery County Rescue came out on their ATV and picked him up to take to a
> hospital. It turns out that they each had cell phones, but their
> batteries were dead. They were a nice young couple, taking a semester off
> from school to hike the AT between their junior and senior years. They
> were from Concord, MA and he was a chemistry major at Villanova.
>
