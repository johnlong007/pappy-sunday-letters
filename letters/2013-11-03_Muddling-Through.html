November 3, 2013 · Sunday

Muddling Through

17 photographs·710 words

While we were in England I learned that "muddling through" means to carry
through to a successful conclusion in spite of apparent lack of planning or
direction.

Vehicle report:

Finally got the VW problem resolved I believe. It is difficult when one
installs new (but defective) parts. After a lot of time, effort,
aggravation and ,by my standards, money. I went back to the beginning and
put in new wheel bearings.

There was no progress on the '37 this week. But, quite a bit on the MM. It
is pretty much all bolted back together now. Dwight measured to have an
exhaust pipe fabricated. A trial run should be forthcoming.

Speaking of old tractors, Don Haas is also a retired ECC engineer (Chem E.
from UT who also got his masters from the univ. of Wash.). Don likes
IHequipment and has a bunch. He also has a picture book farm and home
in
Greene county. He recently purchased a corn binder and a corn shredder.
Since I had never seen either in operation, I went down to watch the check
out.

The Long's may want to rent some of his equipment.

I forgot to mention do-gooder work. The big news there is that Dave felt
well enough to get out. We went back to do lawn work for Jean and Charlie.
I think it is mainly to go listen to lonely old people. (Everyone take
note!).
Dave has tried all sorts of things to help with his Lyme disease. A heat
sauna, a machine to hang him upside down and now he is anticipating in a
group teleconference to learn to teach his cells to kill bacteria. Mind
over matter. I will stay tuned with interest.

AT work was fun. South bounders are going through. One of them was Darrell.
He lives in upstate NY. About 65 miles North of Rochester. I talked with
him for a few minutes. Then later another fellow came through and told me
more about Darrell . He hikes in blue jeans, always. Does 35 miles/day.
Carries a 12 pound pack and is an ultra marathoner,(whatever that is). All
the South bounders have the end in sight and all the weak and faint hearted
have long since dropped out.

Once more I spent all my time digging trail. I came to a big stump. I
attacked it with my bladed mattock . Directly, Kim showed up with a small
saw. We were working away but decided it was quitting time and we were too
frazzled to finish the job. Then, some of the guys who had been further
down the hill came trudging up the trail I told Kim to keep working and we
will do the Tom Sawyer bit.

The leaves are past their peak why up on top of the mountains. But, I took
a picture anyway for the benefit of those who may have forgotten. Then, I
took some more around here.

The big news here is that Allison was home and put in a request for B & G.
I believe I see a trend developing.

You can see a pot of pears on the stove. GSH has worked up a lot of pears
and converted a lot of Herb's apples into pies this week.

Today, we went for another dinner cruise on the lake in the HMS D&D. It was
fun but chilly out there, Not as many critters or bright foliage as last
time.

I did take a picture of the captain eating but all he had was a turnip.

Speaking of turnips, we have a fine crop. Plenty for everyone.

Lest we forget, the cat is doing fine. He has been moved into the shop for
the last few nights. Still likes GSH much more than he likes me but some
progress has been made.

Anything exciting going on ? Anywhere?
