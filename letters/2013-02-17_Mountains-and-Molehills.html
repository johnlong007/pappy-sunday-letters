February 17, 2013 · Sunday

Mountains and Molehills

7 photographs·337 words

I spent two full days this week cleaning and documenting armaments.
Now, I am giving a lot of thought to what we can do to protect these
assets. It seems to me our best protection is to keep would be thieves
from being able to tell when we are not home. I am open to (helpful)
suggestions.

I decided it is probably better that I do not include pictures of the
collection as I do not really trust Mr. Google.

For the past few weeks the AT crew has been working on a relocation at
Chestoa. We finished and opened it for business on Tuesday. We leave
the ends which connect to the existing trail until the last thing. I
am attaching pictures of the part I worked on Tuesday. If you look
carefully, you should be able to see the markers in the woods on the
left followed by the completed trail in the next picture.

The Friday hikers used the trail . It is four miles from Chestoa to
the Curly Maple Gap shelter. That is a shelter we tore down to the
ground and rebuilt three years ago. The first time I have been back
since then I believe.

The relocation met with their approval.

Saturday I went with Dwight to Seymour to visit pat and the racecar to
be. You can see by the picture what goes on during these visits. BUT,
if you remember the door handles on the '37 were not correct. Too
short. Pat welded and extension on each and I now have door handles.

Today We went to the Baptist church worship service.

All my life I have heard my grandmother say "there are more ways to
kill a cat than choking it to death on butter". Some time back I came
across a book "The Hoosier Schoolmaster" which is a contrived novel to
use all the expressions of the Appalachian mountains and western
frontier . It was published in 1871. I was delighted to read the
pictured page.
