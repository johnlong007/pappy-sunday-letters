August 19, 2012 · Sunday

Diamonds and Rust

13 photographs·929 words

The string trimmer pull quit engaging the engine. After watching an online
video (and with GSH supervising), I got it all taken apart. Turns out the
plastic drive gear is worn out. Another stay tuned project.

The HP 1600 Laser Jet printer quit printing and gave a "51.23" error
message. I was able to snabble Wayne who undertook to follow the online
instructions for exposing the printer innards and cleaning the laser jets
etc. It was fun ,in a perverted manner, and interesting as that is a very
complex piece of equipment with thousands of parts and pieces. Anyway, he
took it apart, cleaned and put it all back together with no broken parts or
parts left over.

But, it still does not work. Still "51.33"

The Ferrari "THE CAR" is on the third owner. It has had a multitude of
drivers most recently a rash of young relatively inexperienced
driver/operators. Aaron came over one day bringing his Dad along per my
invitation. I asked Aaron if her would like to take his Dad for a ride. In
due time, they returned with Ernie driving. When they came in Aaron sort of
sidled around then announced "we broke your Ferrari". It appears that Ernie
had for some reason attempted to turn the headlights on by pulling back on
the turn signal lever. It broke.

I like to go check on the pool water from time to time. Monday I checked
and found the pump not running and the water turning green. (Photo upon
request). While Wayne was here, we pulled the motor/pump unit and took
apart. The motor is frozen and smells cooked. I delivered it to Dwight for
autopsy. Once more, stay tuned.

I have been blaming the damaged corn crop on our resident crow population.
One morning I went out and saw 7 yearling, long legged, long necked,
turkeys out in the corn patch. While I watched one jerked and ear off the
stalk and proceeded to peel shuck back and peck off the kernels. I turned
the dogs out . They rushed out and put the turkeys to flight. Later, I went
down the drive on the tractor and found the turkeys happily having brunch
in the lower garden.

Enough rust?

I think I need to keep on helping Katy learn the role of the GSH. It
involves being willing to cheerfully drag dead cars and/or tractors around
as operator of either . The tractor being eased down the driveway is a Case
"SC". I purchased it from a person just outside the park at Gatlinburg
around 1996. It was running fine when I parked it in the barn. Now, things
to various willing workers, it has been cleaned and painted. Just needs to
be buttoned up and should be ready for a second life. Anyone need/want a
red tractor?

Do family projects count as do-gooder? Anita put in a plaintive call for a
saw operator. Seems a dead locust had fallen on her stored picket fence. As
bait, she offered a brat for supper.

PBBD reported, with pictures, on the fine event at her house. For those who
missed it, I will include my view on the party. I got to talk with most of
the students and with both Aaron and Vanessa.

I went out for AT work three days this week. Monday Dave asked me to go
help clear a large downed tree on his section near Watuga lake. Brian Paley
(a recent import from Canada) also helped. I shall include a before and
after picture.

Tuesday, I went with Brian and Bob Peoples to finish the rock step project
we had started last week. It was fun, I chipped and split large stone steps
to order. I shall include a picture of our finished project. Some of the
rocks would weigh over 200 pounds.

Friday we had 100 ASU students come in by buss to backbone rock to work on
digging relocations for the steep trail going up to the AT.

First, I will include a picture of my group going up the steps we have
worked on for the last two weeks.

Of course, I took a lot of pictures of workers during the day. Those are
available upon request. But, for comic relief, I think I will include one I
took of Pat Loven during lunch break. Sis may remember Pat, he went with us
on the mission to Ecuador. His wife, Karen is a remarkable person who is
now getting her hair back after undergoing chemo for BC. They have a son
who teaches English hither and yon. He is currently in Inner Mongolia.
Karen and I are going to visit him next year.

I made it to Erwin one day to check on Herb after his surgery then Saturday
he came by here. Looks like he is healing well.

Today, we drove to Limestone to check on Nancy and Lewis. Nancy is doing
well. She likes country living and has a flock of maybe 50 chickens running
loose. She has slimmed down a bit and taken wrinkle removal action. Looks
good. Lewis has gained weight, had knee replacement surgery and is barely
mobile. Is a was a good visit.

Oh, a picture of Backbone Rock . One has to be on top to appreciate why it
is so named. It on the road between Shady Valley and Demascus.

Enough !

It would be nice if some of the college kids would report . I assume no
news means they are spending a lot of time in the library.
